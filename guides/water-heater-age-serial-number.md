# How Old Is My Water Heater? Read the Serial Number by Brand

A water heater's serial number is printed on the label fixed to the tank, the one that also carries the model number, capacity and working pressure. A.O. Smith calls it the nameplate on the side of the tank; Bradford White calls it the rating plate. Photograph the whole label and copy the serial exactly, letters included, because the first few characters are the date and the same characters mean different dates on different brands.

For a house you're buying, the age is something to get in writing during your inspection period, not something to guess from how the tank looks. A water heater's serial number gives the year the heater was made, which is close to, but not always the same as, the year it went in.

## A.O. Smith water heaters: year first, then the week

A.O. Smith's July 2024 service guide for its contracted service providers reads the serial number from the left. The first two digits are the year of manufacture and the next two are the week, and the guide notes that "There can be up to fifty-three (53) weeks in a given manufacturing year." Its own example serial starts 17 then 19, which the guide reads as the nineteenth week of 2017.

A.O. Smith doesn't put its year-then-week serial rule on its consumer pages. What the consumer site offers instead is a warranty verification page that takes the serial number, which is the route to use when the digits don't fit the pattern.

## Rheem and Ruud water heaters: check the first character

Rheem's two public explanations of its serial numbers don't agree at first sight. Rheem's serial-number page says a ten-digit serial reads month manufactured, then year manufactured, using the example 12 09 D 01234. A 2022 post on Rheem's blog says the first few digits give "the week and year of manufacture." Technical bulletin 1207, which covers Rheem, Ruud and Richmond serial numbers, shows that both are right for different labels.

The change in Rheem bulletin 1207 is dated: from April 2, 2012 at the Mexicali plant, and June 4 at Nuevo Laredo, heaters got a new rating label with a serial that starts with a plant letter, then two digits for the week, then two for the year. The bulletin's example, M141209135, is week 14 of 2012 from Mexicali. The plant letters it lists are A for Montgomery, W for Water Heater Innovations, Q for Nuevo Laredo, M for Mexicali and C for Canada, and it says each plant moved to the new numbers on its own schedule.

For a Rheem or Ruud water heater, the first character of the serial decides how to read the rest. A serial that starts with a digit is the older month-then-year format. A serial that starts with one of those plant letters is week-then-year.

## Bradford White water heaters: two letters and a 20-year question

Bradford White codes the date as two letters at the start of the serial: a year letter, then a month letter, with A for January through M for December and no I. The catch is in the year letter. Each letter stands for more than one year, and Bradford White's own two charts don't list the same pairs. Its For The Pro page gives A as 1984 or 2004, running to Z as 2003 or 2023. Its consumer My Water Heater page gives A as 2004 or 2024, running to Z as 2023 or 2043. Read together, an A could mean 1984, 2004 or 2024.

The rest of the Bradford White serial narrows it down. The pro page says the production number after the two letters is "seven digits in length before December 2007 (DM), and eight digits in length after." By our reading of the two charts, an A followed by eight digits can only be the newest of the three years, while seven digits still leaves two decades to choose between. At that point, enter the serial on Bradford White's My Water Heater Information page, which the company says gives the manufactured date. One known oddity: Bradford White says some heaters carry an OA serial because of a computer error, and those were built in January 1997.

## The same four digits, three different dates

A serial can't be read without knowing the brand. We took the digits 0912 and read them under each maker's published rule:

| Serial starts with | Rule it follows | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| 0912 on an A.O. Smith | year, then week | Week 12 of 2009 |
| M0912 on a newer Rheem or Ruud | plant letter, week, then year | Week 9 of 2012 |
| 0912 on an older Rheem or Ruud | month, then year | September 2012 |

Identical serial digits land three years apart depending on the brand name on the water heater. If a listing, a seller or an online decoder gives you an age without saying which brand rule it used, check it against the label yourself.

## When a water heater's serial won't decode

The makers' own lookups are the fallback for a serial that doesn't fit, or a brand not covered here. Bradford White's My Water Heater Information page takes the serial and returns the manufactured date. A.O. Smith's warranty verification page takes the serial or a reference number. Ruud has a water heater age calculator.

The Ruud calculator looks up real serials rather than decoding the pattern. When we entered Rheem's own published example, 1209D01234, it answered "It looks like this serial number isn't coming up," and sent us to customer service. A not-found result there means the number isn't in its records, not that the label is wrong.

A permit record can also date the installation. Some places require a permit to replace a water heater; Martin County, Florida, for one, lists water heater replacements among the jobs that still need a permit after the 2026 exemption rules. Our guide to [looking up building permits by address](/guides/look-up-building-permits-by-address/) covers where to search. A permit gives the install date, the serial gives the build date, and a heater can sit in a warehouse between the two.

## How old is too old for a water heater?

No single age settles it, and the sources don't agree on one. A.O. Smith puts the average life expectancy at about 8 to 12 years, and says a heater "more than 10 years old" that leaks around the base of the tank or runs erratically is probably due for replacement. The figure our report uses, from the Department of Energy, is about 15 years for a tank water heater, and individual units vary widely.

We'd treat the age as a reason to look harder, not as a verdict. An 11-year-old tank with a dry base and steady hot water hasn't failed, and a 6-year-old one weeping at the base has a problem whatever its label says. Our report engine puts a water heater replacement at $1,200 – $3,000, which is the number to have in mind if the heater is near the top of either range and you're deciding whether to ask the seller for a credit. Our guide to [negotiating repairs during the option period](/guides/option-period-negotiating-home-repairs/) covers how that request works in Texas.

Ask your inspector to record the manufacture date from the data plate in the report, so the age is in writing rather than in a conversation. The inspector will also look at the relief valve on the tank; our guide to the [TPR valve check](/guides/tpr-valve-inspectors-always-check/) explains what that test shows, and the [expansion tank guide](/guides/water-heater-expansion-tank-required/) covers when the heater also needs an expansion tank.

## How we checked this

On 20 November 2026 we read each maker's own serial-number rules: A.O. Smith's July 2024 Service and Warranty Guide for contracted service providers and its consumer repair-or-replace and warranty pages; Rheem's serial-number page, its 2022 blog post and technical bulletin 1207; and Bradford White's For The Pro date-code page, its consumer My Water Heater page and its FAQ. The two PDFs were downloaded with the owner's agreement and read as text. We ran Rheem's published example serial through Ruud's age calculator. The three-way table of the same digits is ours, built from those published rules, and the lifespan and replacement cost come from our own report engine.

What we couldn't check: serial formats for other brands, because we didn't read a primary source for them; any real heater's serial, because every real serial belongs to someone's home; and which year a Bradford White letter means beyond what the sequence-length rule can tell, because neither of its charts says.
