# Is Christmas the Best Time to Buy a House? It Depends on Your City.

The advice to buy in December rests on a real pattern: house prices rise in spring and summer and ease off in winter. The question for a buyer is how big that winter dip is where they're looking, and whether Christmas actually falls in it.

So we measured it. The Federal Housing Finance Agency publishes its house price index two ways, with and without the seasonal pattern removed. FHFA's own description of the adjustment is that it removes "recurring calendar year patterns". Comparing the two versions shows how far prices sat above or below the trend purely because of the time of year. We averaged that over ten full years, 2016 to 2025, for the US, its nine census divisions and 100 metro areas.

## The national pattern: January, not December

Nationally, the low point isn't December. It's January: prices on loans made in January sat 0.85% below trend on average, against 0.52% below in December. The peak was June, at 2.18% above. Peak to low, that's about 3 points.

The reason January shows up as the low is how FHFA dates a sale: "we use the loan origination date as the relevant transaction date." A house agreed around Christmas is counted weeks later, when its loan is made. So for this question, the January figure is the closest thing to a Christmas price.

## Your metro

## When the dip comes before Christmas

The table hides a twist that matters more than the size of the dip. In 58 of the 100 metros, the cheapest quarter was October to December. In 42 it was January to March.

That difference decides whether a Christmas deal lands in the low. Take Seattle. Loans made from October to December sat 2.22% below trend, the deepest winter low of any metro in the table above. But loans made from January to March, which is where a deal agreed over the holidays gets counted, sat 1.04% above trend. Denver follows the same shape. In those markets, the seasonal low falls on deals struck in the autumn, and by the time a Christmas contract becomes a loan, prices are already back above trend. Across all 100 metros, 25 had January-to-March prices above trend.

Detroit, Boston, Philadelphia and New York run the other way, with the first quarter as their lowest. There, the January loan, and so the Christmas deal, is the one that sits closest to the bottom.

## What a couple of points is worth

On a $400,000 house, the median dip of 2.29 points is about $9,160. Seattle's 4.89 points would be about $19,560; Miami's 0.79, about $3,160. That's our arithmetic on a round number, not a forecast. It describes an average pattern across thousands of sales, and any one seller can sit far from it in either direction.

## What this data can't tell you

- **Whose sales it covers.** FHFA's index is built from loans purchased or securitized by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. In FHFA's words, that data gives broad coverage but does "not encompass all residential mortgage transactions." FHFA publishes a separate expanded index to take in cash sales and loans from other sources, which tells you the main one leaves them out.
- **Why prices dip.** The index measures prices, not motives. It doesn't tell you whether winter sellers are more flexible, or whether there are fewer houses to choose from.
- **Odd years.** Ten years includes the pandemic years. An average over a decade smooths any single year out; it doesn't remove it.

If you do end up buying in winter, the inspection has its own seasonal limits; we've set out [what snow can hide from a home inspection](/guides/snow-hide-winter-home-inspection/).

## How we checked this

On 17 November 2026 we downloaded FHFA's master house price index file and ran our own script over it. The script keeps the purchase-only index, compares the seasonally adjusted and unadjusted values for each month or quarter, and averages the gap over 2016 to 2025. It skips any metro without all 40 quarters. The script (scripts/analyze-fhfa-seasonality.py) and its output (docs/data/fhfa-seasonality-2016-2025.json) are in our repository.

We also read FHFA's index FAQ, last updated October 7, 2026, for how the index dates sales, which loans it covers and how its seasonal adjustment works.

What we couldn't use: private listing and sales data from the big property portals. Redfin's terms reserve all rights to its data, and realtor.com blocked our visit, so every figure here comes from FHFA's public file.
