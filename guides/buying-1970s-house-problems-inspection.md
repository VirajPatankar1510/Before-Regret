# Buying a 1970s House? Hidden Problems and Inspection Checks

*Built in the 1970s* sounds like one era. For an inspection, it's closer to three. Materials changed partway through the decade, and so did a federal rule, so a house from early in the decade and one from 1979 can share a floor plan and almost none of the same questions.

So pin down the build year before anything else. Listings round it, and county records differ on whether they log the start or the finish. Our [year-built lookup guide](/guides/when-was-my-house-built-by-address/) covers where to find it; the rest of this page assumes you have it.

## The decade in three parts

This comes straight from our report engine. We ran every build year from 1970 to 1979 through the rules that apply anywhere in the country, then grouped the years where the result changes. Local rules, such as foundation type in clay-soil counties or termite pressure in the South, add to it where they apply.

### Built 1970 to 1973

These are the years with the most on the list. Our engine looks for **aluminum branch wiring** in houses from 1965 through 1973. The Consumer Product Safety Commission's repair booklet puts the window at "between 1965 and the mid 1970s", and includes additions and rewired circuits. So a house from the middle of the decade isn't automatically clear, and neither is an older house with a 1970s addition. If the inspector finds it, the question becomes what the repair costs and what to ask the seller for. That's covered in [aluminum wiring repair cost](/guides/aluminum-wiring-repair-cost-before-closing/).

The same years carry the **cast iron sewer** check. Cast iron was common for drain lines in houses built before the early 1970s, and a house this age may still have its original line. Nobody can see it without a camera, and a general inspection usually doesn't include one. Our [sewer camera guide](/guides/sewer-scope-before-buying-house/) walks through the four findings that matter.

### Built 1974 to 1977

The list gets shorter. What stays is the **lead-paint disclosure**, which applies to housing built before 1978. In practice that means a pamphlet, a signed form listing whatever lead paint the seller is aware of, and copies of past lead reports if any exist. Nobody has to test. You're entitled to 10 days to arrange your own lead test unless the contract sets a different period, and you can give that up in writing.

The part buyers miss comes after closing. If you plan to repaint, replace windows or open walls, EPA's renovation rule applies to the contractor you hire for a house built before 1978. Anyone paid to disturb painted surfaces has to be certified in lead-safe work practices, "even sole proprietorships". Doing the work yourself in your own home generally isn't covered by that rule. Ask any contractor you're quoting for their certification before the house is yours, so the renovation estimate already includes it.

### Built 1978 and 1979

Two things change. The lead-paint disclosure usually drops away, and **polybutylene** supply pipe comes in. Our engine looks for polybutylene in houses from 1978 through 1996. It's usually something a general inspector can identify at the water heater and the main shutoff, so ask for the pipe material in writing. Here's [what polybutylene looks like](/guides/spot-polybutylene-pipes-before-buying-house/) in a utility room.

The edge case is a house that straddles New Year 1978. EPA and HUD read "housing constructed before 1978" by when it was permitted, so a 1977 permit still brings the disclosure even if the keys were handed over in 1978. Our [year-built guide](/guides/when-was-my-house-built-by-address/) shows how to find that permit.

## The checks that cover all ten years

Some items don't move within the decade, so they're easy to forget once the year-specific ones are sorted.

- **The electrical panel's brand.** Our engine asks for it on every house built from 1950 through 1989, because certain brands from that span have documented problems with breakers failing to trip. Replacement runs $1,500 – $4,000 in our engine. [Reading the label inside the panel door](/guides/identify-electrical-panel-brand/) takes a minute.
- **Asbestos, but only if you'll renovate.** Floor tile, tile adhesive, ceiling texture and pipe wrap from this era can contain it. EPA's advice is to leave material that's in good condition and won't be disturbed alone, and to have a trained, accredited professional take samples before a remodel. Sampling is $200 – $600 in our engine. A standard inspection doesn't do it; see [asbestos and the general inspection](/guides/standard-home-inspection-check-asbestos/).
- **Roof and HVAC ages.** Every house in this decade is old enough that the roof, furnace, air conditioner and water heater have likely been replaced at least once. Ask for the dates in writing.

## Where the 1970s houses are

We counted this from the Census Bureau's American Community Survey (2020–2024, table B25034). Of 143,775,355 housing units in the 50 states and DC, 20,359,888 were built in the 1970s, or 14.2%. That's roughly one in seven. Those counts include apartments and condos, not only houses. In 751 counties, the 1970s is the busiest single decade on record.

By share, the 1970s weigh most in Hawaii (22.7%), Alaska (22.2%) and Wyoming (19%). By sheer number, California, Florida and Texas lead, with 2,458,155, 1,623,493 and 1,590,340 units. Among counties with at least 50,000 housing units, the highest shares are in Anchorage (27.6%), Broward County, Florida (25.8%) and Pinellas County, Florida (25.4%).

In those two Florida counties, about a quarter of the housing is from this decade. That makes the permit history worth pulling before you make an offer: [Broward's permit search](/guides/check-building-permits-broward-county-fl/) and [Pinellas County's](/guides/check-building-permits-pinellas-county-fl/) both show what's been replaced and when.

- **What we ran, on 17 November 2026:** every build year from 1970 to 1979 through our report engine, and a count of the Census table by decade. Both come from one script in our repository, `scripts/build-1970s-housing-figures.ts`, which writes the figures file named above.
- **What we read:**
  - on 17 November 2026: EPA's pages on the renovation rule and on asbestos at home, and CPSC Publication 516;
  - on 1 November 2026, while writing our year-built guide: the lead-disclosure regulation (40 CFR part 745) and the EPA-HUD interpretive guidance.
- **What we're not claiming:** that any 1970s house has any of these materials. A year is a reason to ask, and plenty of these houses have been rewired or repiped since. We also left out claims we couldn't source to a primary document, such as when insulation standards changed.
