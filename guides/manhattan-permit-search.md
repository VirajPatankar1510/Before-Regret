# Manhattan Building Records: DOB Permits and HPD Violations

Most people buying in Manhattan are buying an apartment in a co-op or condo building, not a house, and that changes which records matter. The unit has its own history of alterations, but the building has a longer one, kept by two different city agencies, and a buyer usually needs both.

## Two agencies, two records

**The Department of Buildings** holds the construction record: permits and job filings, inspections, the certificate of occupancy, and DOB and OATH/ECB violations. Manhattan uses the same two systems as every other borough, the Building Information System (BIS) for older filings and DOB NOW for current ones. Our [Bronx guide](/guides/check-building-permits-bronx-ny/) walks through searching both by address or block and lot, and the steps are identical here.

**HPD**, the city's housing agency, holds the other half: how the building has been kept up. Every building of three or more apartments must register with it, co-ops and condos included, and its public site lists what tenants have complained about, what inspectors have cited and whether the owner has been taken to housing court. Our guide to [reading a building's HPD record](/guides/hpd-online-check-nyc-building-violations/) goes through that page line by line.

The two answer different questions. DOB tells you what work was approved and whether it was signed off. HPD tells you how the building has been run.

## What to pull for a Manhattan apartment

- **The building's certificate of occupancy,** from DOB. It sets the legal use and the number of units. An apartment that does not exist on the CO, or a duplex the CO counts as two units, is a question for your lawyer before closing.
- **Alteration filings for the unit.** Renovations that moved plumbing, gas or walls are normally filed with DOB under the building's address by a licensed professional. Ask the seller for the job numbers rather than hunting through every filing on a large building.
- **The building's open DOB and HPD violations.** A few old minor items are normal in a large building. Open immediately hazardous HPD violations, an active vacate order, or a building in HPD's Alternative Enforcement Program are not.
- **The landmark record, if there is one.** Much of Manhattan sits inside historic districts. Exterior work on a landmarked building also needs a Landmarks Preservation Commission permit under Administrative Code § 25-305, a separate record covered in our [Brooklyn guide](/guides/check-building-permits-brooklyn-ny/), and its permit and violation searches work the same way for Manhattan addresses.

## Why the building's record is your record

In a co-op you buy shares in the corporation that owns the building, so its violations, litigation and repair bills are, in a real sense, yours. In a condo you own the unit outright, but the common charges pay for the building's repairs. Either way, an open facade or boiler problem in the records today can become an assessment later. Ask the managing agent, through your lawyer, what each open item is and what it will cost to clear.
