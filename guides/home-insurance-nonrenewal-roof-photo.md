# Home Insurance Nonrenewal After a Roof Photo? What Homeowners Can Do

A nonrenewal notice comes before the policy ends, often by a period state law sets, and that time is the whole game. A notice isn't the same as losing coverage today, and it doesn't have to be the final word. The steps below are in the order they pay off. Most of them cost nothing but a phone call and some paperwork.

If you want to know why aerial images flag roofs that are fine, and what state regulators have said about it, [our guide to roofs flagged by aerial photos](/guides/home-insurance-aerial-photos-roof/) covers that. This page is about what to do once the letter is in your hand.

## 1. Read the notice and pin down the reason

Find three things in the letter: the date the policy ends, the stated reason, and what the insurer relied on. Some states require all three to be spelled out.

- **Texas:** under Insurance Code § 551.002, a written statement of reasons must state "the precise incident, circumstance, or risk factors" and "the source of information on which the insurer relied." Section 551.105 adds that unless the insurer mails written nonrenewal notice at least 60 days before the policy expires, it must renew at your request.
- **Florida:** Florida Statutes § 627.4133(2)(b) requires written notice at least 120 days before a residential policy's nonrenewal takes effect. Florida § 627.7011(5) also bars an insurer from refusing to renew "solely because of the age of the roof" when the roof is less than 15 years old.
- **Pennsylvania:** the Insurance Department's Notice 2024-06 says that for policies in force 60 days or more, insurers must give "both the legal and factual reason" for ending coverage.

If your notice is vague, write back and ask for the specific reason and the source in writing. Your state's insurance department can tell you what a notice must include where you live.

## 2. Ask for the image

Ask the insurer, in writing, for a copy of the image the decision was based on, the date it was taken, and confirmation of which address and structure it shows. Old images, blurry ones and pictures of the wrong building are among the problems state regulators have found when reviewing complaints, as [the aerial photo guide](/guides/home-insurance-aerial-photos-roof/) describes. You can't answer an image you haven't seen.

## 3. Build your own evidence

Get a roofer or a home inspector to look at the roof and give you a short written report, with photos that carry the date. Pull together the roof's paper trail too: the installation invoice, any warranty registration, and the permit for the last roof replacement if your town required one. If the notice cites roof age, a documented install date is your strongest answer. If it cites something that is really there, such as branches over the roof or debris, deal with it and photograph the result.

## 4. Ask for a physical inspection or a second look

Send your evidence to the insurer with a request to reconsider, and ask for a physical inspection. Pennsylvania's notice puts the regulator's view plainly. Images showing only discoloration, streaking or other cosmetic issues "should not be used as the sole evidence to support cancellation or nonrenewal actions." Absent unequivocal and material damage, "it would be prudent for an insurer to conduct a physical inspection." It also says homeowners should have "the opportunity to challenge aerial imagery results or correct confirmed roof deficiencies." Even outside Pennsylvania, that is a reasonable thing to ask for.

Keep a log of every call, email and letter: dates, times, names. You'll need it if you take the next step.

## 5. Use your state's appeal or complaint process

**A hearing, where your state offers one.** Maine is an example. When an insurer proposes not to renew a homeowners policy, the named insured can request a hearing from the Bureau of Insurance. The request must be received within 30 days of getting the notice. The insurer "is required to continue your coverage from the date of your hearing request until the end of the hearing process," and if you win, the policy is reinstated or renewed. Check whether your state has anything similar, and note the deadline.

**A complaint, available everywhere.** The National Association of Insurance Commissioners explains that you can file a complaint with your state department of insurance, including over a "cancellation or nonrenewal that you believe is unjustified." The department forwards it to the insurer, which has to respond. Attach the notice, the image if you got it, your roofer's report and your log.

## 6. Meanwhile, don't let coverage lapse

Start shopping for a replacement policy as soon as the notice arrives, not after the appeal. If your mortgage contract requires insurance and coverage lapses, the servicer can place a policy itself and charge you for it. Federal rules (12 CFR 1024.37) require the servicer to send written notice at least 45 days before charging for it. If you then show proof of continuous coverage, it must cancel that policy and refund any overlap within 15 days. That's a safety net, not a plan. A replacement policy lined up before your end date is.
