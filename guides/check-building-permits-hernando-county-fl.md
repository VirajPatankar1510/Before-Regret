# Hernando County Building Department: Permit Search

Hernando County's Building Division keeps a house's permits in two places, and the date the permit application went in decides which one. The [Building Division page](https://www.hernandocounty.us/building-development/building/) links both: the New Permit Application Status and Property Search Portal, a Tyler system the county calls current "As of 03/03/2026," and the Legacy Permit Application Status Tool for anything older. For questions, the Building Division is at 789 Providence Boulevard, Brooksville, on (352) 754-4050.

## Which Hernando County system holds a house's permits

The legacy tool holds the older Hernando County permits. In the county's words, it "can be used to view permits and related documents submitted prior to 3/3/26," and it is now for "REVIEW, STATUS AND INSPECTION SCHEDULING ONLY." The Tyler portal holds what came after. The county's FAQ says the public can search that portal "by address, parcel number, or permit number" without logging in, and that the public view shows permit status, project descriptions, inspection results and issued permits.

For a house you're buying, the age of the house tells you where to start. We ran the Census Bureau's year-built estimates for Hernando County's 92,535 homes:

| Built | Share of Hernando County homes | Where the original building permit should be |
|---|---|---|
| Before 1980 | 18.6% | Legacy tool, if it was ever entered |
| 1980s | 29.5% | Legacy tool |
| 1990 to 2009 | 42.3% | Legacy tool |
| 2010 or later | 9.6% | Legacy tool, unless the application went in on or after 3/3/26 |

The median Hernando County home was built in 1991, and 90.4% were built before 2010, so almost every resale's original permits sit in the legacy tool. Later work splits by date: a roof or an air-conditioning system permitted last year belongs in the legacy tool, while one permitted this spring belongs in the Tyler portal. Search both before deciding a house has no permit history.

If the house is inside the City of Brooksville, ask the city whether it holds permits for the address as well. The city's website didn't load for us, so we can't describe its records.

## Work that can legally leave no Hernando County permit

An empty result for recent work doesn't always mean the work skipped a permit. On August 6, 2026, Hernando County published an expanded list of residential projects "that homeowners can complete without a permit." The list doesn't apply to commercial property or to "Properties located within a Special Flood Hazard Area (Flood Zone)," so check the flood zone before relying on it.

| Work on a Hernando County house | Permit? |
|---|---|
| Kitchen remodel or new cabinets and countertops, with no new plumbing, electrical or structural work | No |
| Toilets, sinks and vanities replaced in the same place on existing connections | No |
| Water heater, light fixtures, switches, receptacles or smoke alarms replaced like for like | No |
| Drywall repairs up to 8 sheets (256 sq. ft.) | No |
| Roof repairs of 2 squares (200 sq. ft.) or less, nonstructural | No |
| Air-conditioning repair limited to components of the main unit | No |
| A main air-conditioning unit replacement, or any HVAC system replacement | Yes |
| New circuits, an electrical service upgrade, or new plumbing piping | Yes |
| Window and exterior door replacement, other than glass only | Yes |
| Roofing work of 2 squares or more, decks and porches, standby generators | Yes |

A second rule sits on the same county form. From July 1, 2026, the county's notice says an owner of a single-family home or the owner's contractor "may not need to obtain a permit" for work valued under $7500 that involves no mechanical, electrical, plumbing, gas or structural work, though "Exterior doors, windows, roofs and re-roofs still require permits regardless of cost." Projects can't be split to get under the line, and the owner asks for the exemption with the form and a copy of the contract.

The form's last line matters to a buyer: "A local government has no legal duty to the owner, contractor, or successors or assigns thereof for work performed under this exemption." A buyer is a successor. If a seller's bathroom refresh went through as an exemption, ask the seller for the request form and the contract, because the county's permit records may not show it and the county takes no responsibility for the work. We couldn't test whether approved exemptions appear in the Tyler portal.

## When a Hernando County permit was never pulled

Hernando County's FAQ puts unpermitted work on whoever owns the house now. Asked why a new owner has to get a permit for work done before they bought, it answers: "The current owner is ultimately responsible for obtaining a permit for any work performed that would require it even if the unpermitted work was done prior to purchase."

A title search has a limit, and the county FAQ names it. Title companies "should be checking for existing compliance cases prior to closing," but "This only works if the violation was reported prior to the search date." A clean title report tells you no code case was open on that date; it says nothing about a converted garage nobody reported. Our guide to [finding unpermitted work before buying](/guides/find-unpermitted-work-before-buying/) covers the checks that close that gap.

Who did the work matters too. Hernando County "does not have a handyman license," and the FAQ's list of jobs that need no contractor licence is short: plug-in ceiling fans, painting, flooring, like-for-like cabinets and similar work. A handyman's name on a receipt for rewiring or a new water line is a reason to look for the permit.

## What reviving an old Hernando County permit costs

Hernando County adopted a new fee schedule, Resolution No. 2026-037, effective March 3, 2026, the same day the Tyler portal took over. The resolution says one change "updates the technology fee to offset the cost of the new permitting and licensing software"; that fee, applied to all building permits, went from $15.00 to $93.00. The fees a buyer inheriting an unfinished permit is most likely to meet:

- Renew an expired permit: $118.65, plus $47.46 for each remaining or required inspection
- Reactivate a permit: $80.00
- Completion permit: $118.65, plus $80.00 for each required inspection
- Change of main contractor on an issued permit, including owner-builder to contractor: $105.00

As a worked example, an expired permit with 3 inspections still required comes to $261.03 in Building Division renewal fees ($118.65 plus 3 times $47.46). The schedule notes that its fees are the Building Division's only and that other agencies' fees, such as zoning, may apply. In the licensing section of the same schedule, failing to obtain a required permit before work starts carries a $500.00 fine for a first offense, $750.00 for a second and $1,000.00 for a third. Our guide to [resolving open permits before closing](/guides/resolve-open-building-permits-before-closing/) covers who should pay for the fix and when.
