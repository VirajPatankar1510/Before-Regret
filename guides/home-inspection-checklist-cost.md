# Home Inspection: Checklist, Cost and What Inspectors Skip

A home inspection is narrower than the name suggests, and the states that license inspectors write the limits down. Florida's statute describes an inspection on a home as "a limited visual examination" of the readily accessible systems: the structure, electrical, HVAC, roof covering, plumbing, interior, exterior, and the site conditions that affect the structure. Texas calls it "a limited visual survey and basic performance evaluation." Knowing where an inspection stops tells you what the fee buys and what you'll need to book separately.

In Texas, the home inspection home buyers order during the option period is governed by the inspectors' standards of practice, 22 TAC §535.227 to §535.233, which set the minimum for a one-to-four-family home. Much of that minimum is measurable, so we turned the parts a buyer can check into a table.

## A home inspection checklist built from the Texas rules

Each row of this home inspection checklist pairs what a Texas inspector must do with the point where the rules let them stop. Use it to read the report when it comes back, not as a list to inspect yourself.

| Part of the house | What the inspector must do or report | Where the inspector can stop |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation | Give a written opinion on how the foundation is performing, and say where the crawl space was viewed from | Need not enter a crawl space with under 18 inches of headroom or an opening smaller than 24 inches wide and 18 inches high |
| Roof | Inspect the covering from the roof surface; report water penetration and previous repairs | Can stay off a roof they can't safely reach or stay on; need not give remaining life, the number of layers, or latent hail damage |
| Attic | Report where the attic was viewed from, the approximate average depth of insulation, and water penetration | Need not enter an opening smaller than 22 inches by 30 inches or with under 30 inches of headroom |
| Garage to house | Report a door to the house that is solid wood under 1-3/8 inches thick or not a 20-minute fire-rated door | — |
| Bedrooms | Report any sleeping room without a working emergency escape opening, and missing smoke alarms in each sleeping room | Need not verify that the smoke alarms are interconnected |
| Electrical panel | Report a panel without 36 inches of clear space in front, missing dead fronts, and the GFCI and arc-fault protection the rules list room by room | Need not judge whether the service capacity is enough |
| Water supply | Report the static water pressure, where the main shutoff is, and pressure over 80 PSI | Need not operate main shutoff valves or judge water quality |
| Drains and septic | Report drain performance and active leaks | Need not look for sewer clean-outs or inspect a private sewage system |

The GFCI part of the Texas inspection rules names 11 locations and the arc-fault part names 13, which is why an older house's electrical section can run long. Our guide on [GFCI outlets in garages and basements](/guides/need-gfci-outlets-pass-home-inspection/) covers what an inspector's GFCI note means for a house built before those requirements.

Pools, spas, sprinkler systems and outbuildings sit in a separate optional section, 22 TAC §535.233, and the rules say an inspector is not required to inspect anything in it. If the house has a pool, a spa, a sprinkler system or an outbuilding, get it in writing that the inspection includes them before you pay.

## Where a home inspection can stop short

A Texas inspector can skip a required item in four situations: you both agree to leave it out, the inspector isn't qualified for it, conditions prevent the inspection or make it hazardous or risk damage, or it's a shared part of a multifamily building that doesn't touch the unit. The rules ask for notice in return. The inspector must "notify the client at the earliest practical opportunity" and write the reason on the report, and an inspector who routinely leaves an item out has to tell you before the inspection, not after.

The equipment list for a standard inspection is shorter than many buyers assume. The Texas rules say an inspection doesn't require thermal imaging, moisture meters, gas or carbon monoxide detection equipment, drones, a camera inside the drain or sewer line, or a ladder that reaches higher than one story. An inspector may go further with the competency to do it, so ask which of those tools yours actually brings.

After the inspection, home buyers have a report and a deadline. Our guide on [when it's reasonable to walk away](/guides/when-reasonable-walk-away-after-inspection/) helps with that decision.

## What a home inspection won't tell you

Several answers a buyer might expect from a home inspection fall outside the Texas minimum. The rules say the inspector isn't required to determine a system's "life expectancy or age," its "compliance with any code," or its "insurability or warrantability," and isn't required to "provide repair cost estimates." Termites, asbestos, lead-based paint and mold are outside the inspection too; our guides on [termites](/guides/standard-home-inspection-include-termites/) and [asbestos](/guides/standard-home-inspection-check-asbestos/) cover the separate checks.

Descriptions of the inspection job don't all agree. The American Society of Home Inspectors' 2021 consumer article says your inspector "will look for code violations" and will check the appliances and "estimate how old they are." A thorough inspector may well offer both. In Texas they're beyond the legal minimum, so if code issues or ages matter to you, ask whether they'll be in the report.

Our own report puts the age question more loosely than the Texas inspection rules do. It asks buyers to get the roof's remaining life and the ages of the heating, cooling and water heater in writing, and describes both as "Included in a general inspection." The Texas roof section says the inspector isn't required to determine "the remaining life expectancy of the roof covering." Ask for both when you book, and ask the inspector to copy the manufacture dates from the data plates. Our guides on [reading a water heater's serial number](/guides/water-heater-age-serial-number/) and [telling how old a roof is](/guides/how-to-tell-how-old-a-roof-is/) show what you can check yourself.

## How much should a home inspection cost?

We found no government survey of home inspection cost. The nearest published figure comes from the American Society of Home Inspectors, whose 2021 consumer article says "Basic home inspections are typically priced between $300 and $600" and names what moves the price: the size of the home, the specialized tests it needs and the zip code. Treat that as a trade association's range from five years ago, not a quote. Written quotes from two or three licensed inspectors for the same house will tell you more.

The base fee is only part of the home inspection cost. Checks the standard leaves out, such as a sewer camera scope, a termite report, a radon test or a Level 2 chimney inspection, are priced separately, and our guide on [why not to skip the home inspection](/guides/dont-skip-the-home-inspection/) lists the ranges our report uses for each.

Florida adds a rule about how the inspection fee works. An inspector there can't take a job where "the fee payable for the inspection, is contingent upon" the report's conclusions or the close of escrow, so expect to pay whether or not you buy.

## Checking the inspector before the home inspection

A Florida home inspector needs a state licence, and the inspection law spells out what it takes: an exam, a course of at least 120 hours on the same systems the definition names, fingerprints for a background check, and a commercial general liability policy of at least $300,000. The same law bars an inspector from repairing a home their company inspected, from inspecting a property they have a financial interest in, and from paying a broker or agent for referrals.

We ran Florida's licence search, the Department of Business and Professional Regulation's Verify a Licensee page, for the Home Inspector licence type with Martin County selected. It returned 209 records, each with a licence number starting HI and a status such as "Current, Active". The page notes: "For additional information, including any complaints or discipline, click on the name." Before an inspection, that click on your inspector's name is worth the minute it takes.

In Texas, the inspection standards bind inspectors licensed under Chapter 1102 of the Occupations Code, and "Failure to comply with the standards of practice is grounds for disciplinary action as prescribed by Chapter 1102." That gives you something concrete if your inspector's home inspection report skips a required item without the notice the rules ask for.

## Home inspection for insurance is a different job

An inspection for home insurance is a separate job with its own form. A buyer's home inspection isn't built to answer an insurer's questions; the Texas rules say the inspector isn't required to determine insurability at all.

Florida shows how separate the insurance inspection is. Florida Statutes 627.711 has the Financial Services Commission set a uniform mitigation verification inspection form, used for windstorm discounts, and names who may sign it: a licensed home inspector who has completed at least 3 hours of hurricane mitigation training and a proficiency exam, a certified building code inspector, a licensed general, building or residential contractor, a professional engineer, an architect, or anyone else the insurer recognizes as qualified. The signer must inspect the house "personally, not through employees or other persons," though engineers and contractors may send a direct employee. And as with buyers' agents, the inspector can't pay an insurance agent for referrals.

Florida's licence search doesn't show that hurricane mitigation training. Its Special Qualification filter for home inspectors offers only "Home Inspector" and "Temp Home Inspector", so when you book an inspection for home insurance in Florida, ask the inspector for proof of the training along with the licence number. For the 4-point inspection, see our guide on [what to do when a Zinsco panel fails one](/guides/will-zinsco-panel-fail-4-point-inspection/).
