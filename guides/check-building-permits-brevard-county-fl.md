# Brevard County Permit Search: Find a House's Permits

Brevard County splits a house's building permits between two search tools by date, and some Brevard houses' permits aren't with the county at all. The county's [Search Permits page](https://www.brevardfl.gov/PlanningAndDevelopment/BuildingPermits/PermitSearch) links both tools: Brevard's Advance Service Site, known as BASS, "For permits 2003 to present", and a legacy search for building permits from 1990 to 2002. Anyone can view permit information in BASS without an account. For questions, the county's building permits line is 321-633-2187, and Planning and Development is at 2725 Judge Fran Jamieson Way in Viera.

## Brevard County's three permit eras

| Permit date | Where to search | What the county says it holds |
|---|---|---|
| 2003 to now | BASS (Accela Citizen Access) | Building, code enforcement and contractor records |
| 1990 to 2002 | Legacy Permit Search | "building permits from 1990 to 2002 and other permits from 1990 to 2007", septic permits included |
| Before 1990 | No online search | Ask through a public records request |

The era that matters depends on the work you're checking, not only on the age of the house. We split the Census Bureau's year-built estimates down to the part of Brevard the county permits, unincorporated Brevard plus Grant-Valkaria, which comes to 114,752 homes:

| Built | Share of homes in the county's permit area | Where the original construction permit should be |
|---|---|---|
| Before 1990 | 51.6% | Not online; public records request |
| 1990s | 18.7% | Legacy Permit Search |
| 2000s | 19.0% | Legacy search through 2002, BASS from 2003 |
| 2010 or later | 10.7% | BASS |

Just over half of the homes in Brevard County's permit area went up before its online records begin, so for most resales the permit that built the house won't appear in either tool, and on an older house that gap alone isn't a warning sign. Later work is different. A reroof or an air-conditioning change-out permitted since 1990 should be in the legacy search or BASS, depending on the date the permit was applied for.

## Does Brevard County hold this house's permits?

Brevard's cities run their own building departments. The BASS search page puts it plainly: "Each of the municipalities within Brevard County (except the Town of Grant Valkaria prior to September 17, 2018) are responsible for permitting within their jurisdiction and maintain their own records." The county database covers "unincorporated Brevard County and the Town of Grant Valkaria." Counted in homes, the county's permit area is smaller than its map suggests: by our Census split it holds 38.3% of Brevard's 299,305 homes, and the 15 cities hold the other 184,553.

Don't go by the city in the mailing address. In our BASS search, every permit for the county's own headquarters in Viera carried the address MELBOURNE FL 32940, yet Viera is unincorporated: the Census Bureau lists Viera East and Viera West as unincorporated places, as it does Merritt Island, Port St. John and Mims. Brevard County's FAQ gives the reliable check. Look the house up on the Property Appraiser's site, where the record shows a Taxing District that the county says indicates the jurisdiction. If the district is a city, that city holds the permits.

For a Grant-Valkaria house, search BASS and ask the town as well. The county's note dates a change for the town to September 17, 2018 without saying which permits moved, and we couldn't settle it from the county's pages.

## Searching BASS without losing permits

BASS opens on a General Search form, and three habits keep it from hiding Brevard permits.

Search by account number where you can. The BASS permit page is blunt about it: "For the most accurate search on a property, enter ONLY the Property Appraiser's Account Number." The number is on the same Property Appraiser record that shows the taxing district.

Clear the dates before searching. The Start Date box arrives filled in, and on 20 November 2026 it read 11/16/2021, so a permit search left as it is skips anything older. We set it to 01/01/1990 and searched the county headquarters at 2725 Judge Fran Jamieson Way. BASS reported 100+ records and listed only the newest hundred, reaching back to 2017. Moving the end date to 12/31/2005 brought up 13 records the first search never showed, the oldest dated 04/14/2003. If a permit result says 100+, search again in date slices until each slice shows a real count.

Type less of the address. The county's own advice for BASS permit searches is that "less is best. Only use street number and first few letters of street name. Do not select street type or direction." Our search shows why: the 2003 to 2005 records spell the street WY and the newer ones WAY, so picking a street type would have dropped one set.

![BASS results for 2725 Judge Fran Jamieson Way between 1990 and 2005: 13 records from 2003 to 2005 with older-style numbers such as M0163907](/images/brevard-bass-permit-search-2003-records.webp "Brevard County BASS: records from 2003 to 2005 that the first search didn't list, 20 November 2026")

Older BASS permits look different from new ones. They carry numbers like M0163907 where newer ones read like 26BC15453, longer type names, and statuses such as FINALED in capitals. Brevard's Building search also mixes in records a buyer doesn't need; ours included a bingo licence and a county fire alarm.

Opening a permit shows more than the list does. Record M0163907, a 2004 interior renovation on the county campus, names the licensed contractor with a state licence number, and its Inspections tab still lists six completed inspections, framing, insulation and drywall among them, each with a result date.

## Reading a 1990 to 2002 Brevard legacy permit

The legacy search is a separate Brevard County site with its own form: permit number, contractor, owner name, street number and name, a city drop-down, ZIP code and application dates. The city list holds postal names, not jurisdictions; Merritt Island, Mims, Scottsmoor and Sharpes are on it and Viera isn't, so a permit search by street number and name is the safer start.

A legacy permit record is worth opening. Each one has fields for the application, issue and certificate of occupancy dates, the parcel ID, the construction value and square footage, and a name and licence number for the drywall, electrical, HVAC, plumbing, roofing and septic contractors. For a house built in the 1990s, that's a list of who did the original work. The legacy search covers septic permits too, so look for the septic system's record if the house isn't on sewer.

We couldn't show a typical house result in the legacy permit search. Our only test address was the county headquarters, which returned 4,933 items, the first page all street-naming and addressing applications filed under that address.

## Brevard County permits from before 1990

Brevard County's online permit tools stop at 1990, and no county page says whether older permit records survive. To ask, file a public records request through the county's online form, at PublicRecordsRequest@BrevardFL.gov, or with the Public Records Request Coordinator at (321) 633-2071. Give the address and the Property Appraiser account number and keep the request narrow, because the county warns that "Very broad requests can take longer to fulfill and may become costly." Under Florida law you don't have to identify yourself to ask.

An empty permit search for an old Brevard house tells you the records aren't online, not that the work skipped a permit. For signs of work done without one, our guide to [finding unpermitted work before buying](/guides/find-unpermitted-work-before-buying/) covers what to look for during the inspection period. If the seller did their own work, ask for the permit number: Brevard County says owner-builders "must appear in person to file their permit application," so a seller who pulled a permit will have dealt with the county directly.
