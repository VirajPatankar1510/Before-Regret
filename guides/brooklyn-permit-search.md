# Brooklyn Permit Search: DOB Records and LPC Landmark Permits

A Brooklyn house can have two permit histories, kept by two different city agencies. The Department of Buildings holds the record of building work. If the house is a designated landmark or stands inside a historic district, the Landmarks Preservation Commission holds a second record, of work that changed its exterior and, in some cases, its interior. A buyer who searches only the first can miss the second entirely.

## First, find out whether the house is landmarked

The quickest check is LPC's [Permit Application Finder map](https://www.nyc.gov/site/lpc/applications/permit-application-finder-map.page). Search the address, or click the building, and the map shows whether it is a designated building or site and whether any LPC applications or permits are attached to it.

If it is, the city's Landmarks Law applies to it. Under NYC Administrative Code § 25-305, altering, reconstructing or demolishing a landmark, or a building in a historic district, requires LPC's approval before the work is done. LPC describes its role as working with owners on changes rather than preventing them, but the approval has to come first.

## Searching the house's LPC permits

LPC's [Permit Application Search](https://www.nyc.gov/site/lpc/applications/permit-search.page) looks up applications and permits by:

- address (house number and street name only),
- the name of the landmark or historic district,
- borough, block and lot, with Brooklyn selected as the borough,
- docket number, if you have one.

It covers applications filed and permits issued since January 1, 2016. For anything older, LPC asks you to make a Records Access Request. Read pending results with care: LPC notes that the work types shown on a pending application are what the applicant described, and may not match the scope LPC finally approves.

## Checking for landmark violations

LPC keeps its own enforcement record, separate from DOB's. The [Landmark Violation Search](https://www.nyc.gov/site/lpc/violations/landmark-violation-search.page) shows each violation's status (a Warning Letter, a Notice of Violation or a Summons) and a description of it. Active violations, and those rescinded since January 1, 2020, can also be found on the Permit Application Finder map.

LPC itself says these results are not a substitute for a title search. If something shows up, ask the seller in writing what it was, whether it has been corrected, and how. LPC's Enforcement Department (212-669-7951) can tell you the current status of a violation.

## The DOB side

Building work in Brooklyn is recorded by the Department of Buildings in the same two systems the other boroughs use: the older Buildings Information System for historic filings, certificates of occupancy and violations, and DOB NOW for recent filings. The steps are the same in every borough, and our [Bronx guide walks through both systems](/guides/check-building-permits-bronx-ny/), from finding the block and lot to reading the Property Profile Overview.

On a landmarked house, read the two records side by side. Windows, a stoop, a façade, a roof deck or a rear extension that look recent should have an LPC record from the same period. Where DOB shows the work and LPC shows nothing since 2016, or the reverse, ask the seller when the work was done and whether LPC approved it.

## Before you sign

1. Check the address on the Permit Application Finder map.
2. If it is landmarked or in a historic district, run the LPC permit and violation searches.
3. Pull the DOB history in BIS and DOB NOW.
4. Ask the seller, in writing, about any exterior work that appears in one record but not the other.

Some problems show up in enforcement records rather than permit files, which is why [checking a property's code violations online](/guides/check-code-violations-property-online/) is the other half of the search.

## Looking Up Permits Elsewhere

Outside New York City the agencies and portals change, but the habit is the same: find out who holds the record, then search every system that might. [How to look up building permits by address](/guides/look-up-building-permits-by-address/) covers it for any county.
