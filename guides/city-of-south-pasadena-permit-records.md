# City of South Pasadena Permit Records: How to Get Them

There is no public online search for a South Pasadena house's past permits, so plan to ask for them. The [Building Division](https://www.southpasadenaca.gov/Your-Government/Department-Service-Areas/Community-Development/Building-Division) holds the records. Its Permit Technician answers at (626) 403-7224 or permittech@southpasadenaca.gov. Ask early in your inspection period, because a request takes longer than a lookup, and ask for every permit on the address with its final inspection, not just the recent ones.

## What we found when we looked for a search

South Pasadena moved its permitting online with an OpenGov portal; the city's release is dated August 11, 2026. The portal is built for applying. Its search box finds services like a reroof or solar permit, and when we typed City Hall's address it returned "No results".

The portal does have a page titled "Search for public records". It says you can look up an address to see activity. On 2026-11-24 that page showed the heading and nothing under it: no search box, and no search request in the browser's network log. We'd treat it as switched off for now, not as something you're doing wrong.

Two other dead ends on the city's own Building Division FAQ:

- Under "How can I check if a construction project has a permit?", the FAQ points to a list of active permits that it says is updated on Friday afternoons. The link returned a 404 when we opened it from the city's page. That list only ever covered current projects anyway, not a house's history.
- The FAQ lists "How can I research information on my house?" in its questions at the top, but the page never answers it.

None of this means the records are missing. It means the route to them is a person, not a website.

## How to ask for a house's permit history

Email or call the Permit Technician and give the full street address. A useful request names what you want back: every permit issued for the address, what each one covered, and the date of each final inspection. A permit with no final is the one to raise with the seller. Our guide to [resolving open permits before closing](/guides/resolve-open-building-permits-before-closing/) covers who usually fixes that and when.

If you'd rather put it in writing, the city runs a Public Records Center for records requests. We didn't open its request form, so we can't tell you whether it asks you to sign up first.

Permit issuance runs Monday through Thursday, 7:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., by the Building Division page. One contact block on that same page says Monday to Friday, so phone before you drive to 1414 Mission Street on a Friday.

## You can see the plans, but copying them takes two signatures

Plans on file are worth asking about for any addition, garage conversion or major remodel, because they show what was approved. California Health and Safety Code § 19851 sets the rule for every city building department: the official copy "shall be open for inspection only on the premises of the building department as a public record," and it "may not be duplicated in whole or in part except (1) with the written permission" of the professional who signed the plans and of the original or current owner of the building.

For a buyer, the current owner is the seller. If you want copies, ask the seller to sign during escrow, while they still own the house and still have a reason to help. The city's own route for copies is its Application for Duplicate Official Copy of Plans. Viewing needs no one's permission, so a visit to the counter is the quick option.

## Which of the seller's upgrades should have a permit

The city's FAQ includes a table of common jobs. Here is what it says about the ones buyers usually see in a listing:

| Work on the house | City permit needed? |
|---|---|
| New roof or roof repair, even in the same material | Yes |
| Water heater, new or replacement | Yes |
| Air conditioning, new or replacement | Yes, "even if in exactly the same place" |
| Insulation | Yes |
| Replacement windows in existing openings | Yes |
| Interior remodel with no added square footage | Yes |
| Painting, flooring, kitchen cabinets, rain gutters | No |

Insulation is the row people forget. In a house this old it matters for another reason too: blowing insulation over live knob-and-tube wiring is its own problem, which our [knob-and-tube and insulation guide](/guides/knob-and-tube-wiring-insulation/) explains. If the listing mentions any of the "Yes" work and your request comes back without a permit for it, you have a specific question for the seller. The city's FAQ also states that "State law requires that the seller disclose all known unpermitted construction." For the signs of work nobody pulled a permit for, see [finding unpermitted work before buying](/guides/find-unpermitted-work-before-buying/).

## Old houses here carry a second kind of approval

South Pasadena's housing is much older than the county around it. By our count from Census data, 33% of the city's 10,817 homes were built before 1940, against 14.3% across Los Angeles County. The median South Pasadena home was built in 1953; the county median is 1965. And 87.4% of the city's homes were built before 1980.

Older South Pasadena houses often sit on the city's Cultural Heritage Inventory. The FAQ says the city "has approximately 2,500 properties listed on the Cultural Heritage Inventory, which was established in 1994," and that most homes 50 years or older are listed. Designation only affects exterior changes, but "The City’s regulations require design approval for most exterior changes to homes and buildings."

On an older South Pasadena house, exterior work can have two records: a building permit and a design approval. When you ask about permits, also ask the Planning and Building department whether the house is on the inventory and whether its exterior changes were approved. A new porch, windows or a different roof material on a listed house is exactly where the two records should agree.

If the address turns out not to be inside South Pasadena city limits, these records sit with another city or the county. Our [Los Angeles County permit guide](/guides/check-building-permits-los-angeles-county-ca/) covers the county's own search and the other cities.

## How we checked this

On 2026-11-24 we read the city's August 2026 portal announcement, its Building Division page and its Building Division FAQ, and we used the OpenGov portal and the Public Records Center with no account. The one address we typed was City Hall's, 1414 Mission Street; we don't run private homes through public portals. We checked the wording of Health and Safety Code § 19851 on the Legislature's site that day.

For the age figures we pulled ACS 2020-2024 tables B25034 (year structure built) and B25035 (median year built) for South Pasadena city and for Los Angeles County, and did the division ourselves. Survey figures carry a margin of error, and a house's build year tells you nothing about when it was last permitted.

What we couldn't confirm: whether permits from before the new portal were loaded into it, how long a records request takes, and whether the Public Records Center needs an account. If the city switches on the public records search, a buyer may be able to look a house up directly, and this page will be out of date on that point.
