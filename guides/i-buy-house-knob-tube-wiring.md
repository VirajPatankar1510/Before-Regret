# Can You Buy a House With Knob and Tube Wiring?

Shop for a pre-war house and this question is likely to come up. In our own Census study of housing age, 16,880,456 US homes, 11.74% of the total, were built before 1940. In some states it's close to a third:

| State | Homes built before 1940 |
|---|---|
| District of Columbia | 31.5% |
| Massachusetts | 30.5% |
| New York | 30.3% |
| Rhode Island | 30.1% |
| Pennsylvania | 25.0% |
| Vermont | 24.4% |
| Iowa | 23.5% |
| Maine | 22.9% |
| Illinois | 20.2% |
| Connecticut | 20.2% |

*Source: our study of American Community Survey 2020–2024 data, table B25034.*

Across the country, 582 counties have at least a quarter of their homes built before 1940. Not every one of those houses still has knob and tube, but it's why our reports raise the question for houses from 1955 back.

Finding knob and tube doesn't decide anything by itself. Four answers do, and it pays to get them in this order.

## 1. Is any of it still live?

This answer comes first because everything else depends on it. Your home inspector will usually report the wiring type, but usually isn't required to prove whether it's energized. Washington's inspection standards, for example, don't require the inspector to operate breakers or pull fuses.

An electrician can, and the best result is a signed statement that it's disconnected. [How to find out if it's still live](/guides/is-knob-and-tube-wiring-still-live/) covers what to ask for when only part of the house was rewired. For what knob and tube looks like in the first place, start with [what knob-and-tube wiring is and how to spot it](/guides/what-is-knob-and-tube-wiring/).

## 2. Will an insurer write the policy?

Nobody but the insurer can answer this, and you want that answer on paper for this address. Disclosure plus a signed evaluation from an electrician makes a yes easier. [The insurance page](/guides/get-homeowners-insurance-knob-tube-wiring/) has the checklist for that evaluation.

## 3. Will the lender go ahead?

None of the three big rulebooks, FHA, VA or Fannie Mae, uses the term. We checked FHA's full handbook text word by word. What they care about is the condition of the electrical system. [Our mortgage guide](/guides/mortgage-house-knob-and-tube-wiring/) goes through what each one actually checks.

## 4. If it has to go, what does it cost, and when?

A rewire, partial or whole-house, runs $8,000 – $30,000+ in our report's figures. [Permit fees and the smaller fixes](/guides/knob-and-tube-removal-cost-permits/) are priced on their own page, from published 2026 schedules. Whether the rewire has to happen before closing or can wait is a separate decision, covered in [whether knob and tube must be replaced before closing](/guides/knob-tube-wiring-have-be-replaced-before-closing/).

## Two things that catch buyers later

- **Insulation over the wiring.** Washington, for example, allows insulation over knob and tube only after a licensed electrical contractor has surveyed it and certified it in writing. If the attic is already insulated, ask for that paperwork. See [insulating over knob and tube](/guides/knob-and-tube-wiring-insulation/).
- **Old wiring joined to new.** In a partly rewired house, the joins between the two systems are what inspectors look at closely. See [splicing Romex to knob and tube](/guides/splice-romex-knob-tube-wiring/).
