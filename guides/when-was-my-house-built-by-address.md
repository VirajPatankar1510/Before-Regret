# When Was My House Built? How to Find Out by Address

A house's age is the quickest way to guess what's behind its walls, and if you're buying, it also decides some of the paperwork the seller owes you. The number is easy to find. What it means depends on which record you read and how that county defines it, so this page covers both.

## Where the year is recorded

**The county parcel record.** Every county assessor or property appraiser keeps a record for each parcel, searchable by address, and it shows a year built for each building on the lot. Search for your county's name plus "property appraiser" or "assessor", open the property search, and type the street address.

Counties don't define the field the same way. Sarasota County, Florida's property appraiser defines Actual Year Built as "the year an improvement is completed." Gunnison County, Colorado's assessor records Original Year Built as "the year the construction permit was initiated." One counts from the end of construction, the other from the start. For a house whose construction straddled New Year's Day, the two give different years, and that can matter (see the 1978 section below).

**The building permit history.** The original construction permit, where it survives, is the most direct record of when building started. Later permits show what has been rewired, re-plumbed or added since. Here is [how to look up building permits by address](/guides/look-up-building-permits-by-address/), and if your city uses Accela, our guide to [searching Accela Citizen Access](/guides/accela-citizen-access-permit-search/) walks through it.

**What the Census can't tell you.** Census housing-age tables are built from what residents say on a survey, and the Census Bureau itself warns that the year-built question is "more susceptible to errors of response and non-reporting" than most, because people rely on memory or on estimates from long-time neighbours. Those tables are useful for a county, never for one house.

## Reading the record: what we saw on a real one

We looked up a public building rather than someone's home: the county-owned government parcel at 2000 Main St in Sarasota, on 1 November 2026. This is its Buildings table.

![Sarasota County Property Appraiser record for the county-owned parcel at 2000 Main St, Sarasota: a Buildings table listing six buildings, each with its own Year Built and Eff Yr Built. Building 1 shows Year Built 1926 and Eff Yr Built 2001.](/images/assessor-year-built-vs-effective-year-built.webp)

Two things show up that are easy to misread on a house listing too.

**One parcel, several years.** This parcel has six buildings, and each has its own year. A house with a detached garage, a guest house or a later addition recorded as a separate building can show more than one row. Make sure you're reading the row for the house itself.

**Year Built vs Eff Yr Built.** Building 1 says 1926 in one column and 2001 in the next. The second number is not when it was built. Sarasota defines Effective Year Built as "an appraisal judgment" of a structure's condition and utility, and in Gunnison it stays equal to the original year "until there are significant updates or remodels that extend the life of the structure." A later effective year suggests the assessor judged the building updated. It doesn't say which parts, so the original wiring or plumbing could still be there.

## The 1978 line: when the permit date decides it

For most purposes a year either way doesn't matter. For one federal rule it does.

Under 40 CFR 745.103, housing "constructed prior to 1978" is target housing for the Lead-Based Paint Disclosure Rule, with exceptions for 0-bedroom dwellings, and for housing for the elderly or people with disabilities unless a child under 6 lives there. For target housing, 40 CFR 745.107 requires the seller, before you're bound by the contract, to give you the EPA lead hazard pamphlet, disclose any lead-based paint or hazards they know about, and hand over any records or reports they have. Under 40 CFR 745.110 you also get 10 days to have a lead inspection or risk assessment done, unless you both agree in writing to a different period. You can only waive it in writing.

So what counts as "constructed prior to 1978"? EPA and HUD answered that in their 1996 guidance for real estate professionals, in response to exactly this question about a house started in 1977 and finished in 1978. It means housing "for which a construction permit was obtained (or if no permit was obtained, housing in which construction was started) before January 1, 1978."

That's where the county definitions collide. A Sarasota-style record shows the year the house was *completed*, so a house that says 1978 there may have had its permit issued in 1977. If the record says 1978, look for the original permit before you accept that no lead disclosure is owed. If the permit predates January 1, 1978, ask your agent for the disclosure. The rule doesn't require the seller to test for lead, only to disclose what they know.

The 1970s are where the exact year matters most. The lead-paint line, the start of the polybutylene window and the end of the aluminum-wiring and cast-iron windows all fall inside that decade. Our tally of the Census figures for 3,144 counties puts 14.2% of US housing units in the 1970s and 49.7% before 1980. The Census median year built is 1980 for the country as a whole, but 1958 in the District of Columbia and 1996 in Nevada, so how much of this table applies depends heavily on where you're looking. Our study of [housing built before 1940](/research/oldest-housing/) has the county-by-county figures.

## How we checked this

- **What we read, on 1 November 2026:**
  - the federal lead disclosure rule, 40 CFR 745.103, 745.107 and 745.110 (at Cornell's Legal Information Institute, because the government's eCFR site redirected our automated reader);
  - the EPA and HUD *Interpretive Guidance for the Real Estate Community* of August 20, 1996, question 6;
  - Sarasota County Property Appraiser's glossary;
  - Gunnison County's data definitions for building attributes;
  - the Census Bureau's 2024 American Community Survey subject definitions for year structure built.
- **What we did:**
  - looked up one county-owned parcel in Sarasota and took the screenshot above;
  - added up Census table B25034 (2020–2024) across 3,144 counties, leaving out Puerto Rico;
  - generated the era table from our report engine's own rules.
- **What we left out:**
  - old fire-insurance maps as a way to date pre-war houses (the Library of Congress collection sat behind a bot check, so we didn't read it);
  - where listing sites get their year-built figure;
  - state seller-disclosure forms, which vary.

  If a county's own definition differs from the two quoted here, theirs is the one that applies to that record.
