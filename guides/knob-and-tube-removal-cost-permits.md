# Knob and Tube Removal Cost: Permits, Fees and Fixes

Our reports put a partial to whole-home rewire at $8,000 – $30,000+, and a dedicated electrician evaluation at $150 – $350. The rewire figure moves with the house: wall access, plaster, attic space and circuit count. Those drivers are covered in our [knob and tube basics](/guides/what-is-knob-and-tube-wiring/). This page prices the parts that quotes tend to wrap in one line: the permit, the interim fixes, and the paperwork.

## The permit fee, worked out from two real schedules

Permit fees are public, so you can check this part of a quote yourself. We picked two places where old wiring is common. In our own Census study of housing age, 45.6% of homes in Suffolk County, Massachusetts (Boston) were built before 1940, against 11.74% nationally.

**Los Angeles County (unincorporated areas), schedule effective 1 July 2026.** Alterations to existing homes are charged by branch circuit or by unit. Take a rewire of 15 circuits with a new panel:

| Item | Fee |
|---|---|
| Permit issuance | $41.35 |
| First 10 branch circuits at $25.96 each | $259.60 |
| Next 5 branch circuits at $21.59 each | $107.95 |
| Panel or service, up to 399 amps | $58.35 |
| **Total** | **$467.25** |

If the job needs a plan check, the county adds 70% of the permit fee, with a minimum of $164.02.

**Boston.** When the service is upgraded, the permit is a $20 application fee plus $0.25 per amp: $70 for a 200-amp service, $45 for 100 amps. When the service stays the same, it's $20 plus $1 for each fixture, plug or outlet, so $80 for 60 openings. Only a licensed contractor can pull the permit in Boston.

So the permit is real money, but it's a small slice of the job: $467.25 is 5.8% of the $8,000 low end. If a quote shows a large "permits" line, ask what's in it.

## Skipping the permit costs more than paying it

LA County charges an investigation fee for electrical work done without a required permit. It equals the permit fee, with a minimum of $504.47, or $252.23 when an owner-builder did the work on a one- or two-family home.

As a buyer, the useful version of this is a question for the seller: was the rewire they're telling you about permitted? Our guide to [finding unpermitted work](/guides/find-unpermitted-work-before-buying/) shows how to check the record.

## The cheaper step before a rewire: fuses and breakers sized to the wire

Not every knob-and-tube house goes straight to a rewire. Weatherization programs, which needed old wiring to be safe enough to insulate around, describe a smaller first step:
- **Iowa's 2015 rules** call for S-type fuses in knob-and-tube homes. The rule of thumb is 15 amp fuses for 14-gauge wire and 20 amp for 12-gauge. If S-type fuses won't work because circuits are overloaded, the rules say "a new service panel must be installed and the house brought up to code".
- **The EWEB utilities' form** has the electrician repair defects and install "15-AMP circuit breakers or Type S fuses, if appropriate".

That's the honest shape of the decision. Right-sizing the protection is the inexpensive part. If the circuits are overloaded, it turns into panel work, and then often into rewiring. Neither program publishes a price for the fuse step, and we haven't invented one.

## The letter is a cost too, and it's usually yours

Before any of this, someone has to say what's live. Our report puts a dedicated electrician evaluation at $150 – $350. The EWEB form tells homeowners those costs usually aren't reimbursed by the utility, and "vary significantly from electrician to electrician", so it suggests comparing before you pick one. Our guide to [telling live knob and tube from abandoned](/guides/is-knob-and-tube-wiring-still-live/) lists what that written answer should include.

## Reading a quote

- Ask for the wiring, the wall repair and the panel as separate lines. Then the panel decision is visible.
- Check the permit line against your city's or county's published schedule.
- Ask whether the price covers every circuit that's still knob and tube, or only some. A partial job leaves some old circuits in place.
