# Can You Get a Mortgage on a House With a Fuse Box?

A fuse box on its own is not a reason any of the three big loan rulebooks gives for turning a house down. The questions that matter for the loan are narrower: what turns up on the appraisal walk-through, what condition rating the house ends up with, and whether you can get a fire policy before the closing date. Each has a different fix, and the insurance question takes the longest to answer, so start there.

## What the loan rulebooks say about a fuse box

The FHA, VA and Fannie Mae property rules were searched word for word by Before Regret on 2026-11-26, looking for any rule about fuses, breakers or a minimum amperage for a house being bought.

| Rulebook | Does it mention fuses? | What it does require of the electrical system |
|---|---|---|
| FHA: Handbook 4000.1 (1,871 pages, revised 08/13/2025) | No. "Fuse" and "circuit breaker" appear 0 times | Bare or damaged wiring gets reported, so does a service or panel that looks too small, and some lights, switches and outlets get tried |
| VA: Pamphlet 26-7, Chapter 12 | No. Nor "breaker" or "amp" | Power to each unit; bare or damaged wiring has to be fixed. Nothing is switched on or tested |
| Fannie Mae: B4-1.3-06 | No | The worst condition rating, C6, means a safety or structural problem; it has to be fixed up to C5 or better before Fannie Mae buys the loan |

The FHA handbook mentions amperage on just two of its 1,871 pages. Page 854 is where the appraiser's electrical duties are set out. Page 556 is a renovation-loan work item, covered further down. Rules you may see repeated online, such as an FHA minimum of 60 amps or FHA refusing fuse panels, do not appear in the current handbook.

## Where a fuse box can hold up the appraisal

An FHA appraiser has to note a fuse box whose service looks undersized for the house, so a small fuse service feeding a kitchen full of modern appliances can end up in the report even when nothing is broken. Taking the panel cover off is not part of the appraiser's job. What gets written up is what can be seen: a cover missing, wires showing, a sample of outlets and switches that don't work.

If an FHA appraiser is unsure a house with a fuse box meets HUD's minimum property requirements, the handbook lets the lender call for a further inspection from someone qualified, and faulty or defective electrical systems are on its list of reasons. In practice that means an electrician's letter, and the lender can approve the house only after every defect the appraiser reported has been corrected.

A VA appraisal is lighter on electrical, fuse box or not. Nothing gets switched on or tested, and the utilities can be off on the day; bare or damaged wiring in plain view is the one electrical item VA says has to be fixed. A VA buyer can ask for some required repairs to be waived after the appraisal, but VA's own Topic 44 says waivers should not cover items that could cause safety issues, and exposed wiring is one of those.

For a conventional loan sold to Fannie Mae, the risk is the C6 rating. Under Fannie Mae's rule, one C6 area makes the whole house C6, and the loan can't be sold to Fannie Mae until the problem is fixed and the house rates C5 or better. The guide doesn't name fuse panels, so whether one affects the rating comes down to what the appraiser sees and how it bears on safety.

## Fuse box insurance is the bigger hurdle for the loan

For a conventional mortgage sold to Fannie Mae, the lender must make sure the borrower's property policy meets Fannie Mae's requirements, and section B7-3-02 (08/05/2026) lists the perils every one-to-four-unit policy must cover, and fire or lightning is the first. If a policy leaves out or limits one of those perils, the borrower has to get another acceptable policy that covers it.

Nothing in those insurance sections makes a fuse box uninsurable; the question is whether an insurer will write the house, and that varies by company and state. Our guide to [getting home insurance with a fuse box](/guides/get-home-insurance-fuse-box/) covers what underwriters look at, including over-fusing and 4-point inspections. For the loan, one line in section B7-3-01 matters most: Fannie Mae accepts a policy from a state insurance plan, including a state's FAIR plan, "if they are the only coverage that can be obtained at the time of the loan closing or policy renewal". A FAIR plan quote is a fallback that still lets a conventional loan close; ask your agent whether one is available in your state before you assume you're stuck.

Get the quote before your inspection or option period ends, not in closing week, and tell the agent the house has fuses so the quote is for the house you're actually buying.

## Paying for a new panel

An electrical item flagged on an FHA appraisal has to be corrected before the lender can approve the house, so the work is either done before closing or paid for through a renovation loan. Our report engine's range for a panel replacement is $1,500 – $4,000, and the electrician's own quote, not that range, is the number to negotiate with. If the house also has cloth-covered wiring, our [knob and tube mortgage guide](/guides/mortgage-house-knob-and-tube-wiring/) covers how lenders treat that.

FHA's renovation loan, the 203(k), can fold the work into the mortgage. On a Limited 203(k), total rehabilitation costs may not exceed $75,000, and electrical repair or replacement is an eligible item. On a Standard 203(k), item 28 of the consultant's work write-up requires the electrical service to be raised to 100 amps or the local code minimum, whichever is greater. If you're using a Standard 203(k), plan on a service upgrade, fuses or not.

## What to line up before your contingency ends

- **The amperage, in writing.** Ask your inspector to record the service size and whether the fuses match the wire. A clear note of the service size gives the appraiser less to guess at.
- **An insurance quote that names the fuse box.** If no standard policy is offered, ask about your state's FAIR plan before the option period ends.
- **Your lender's read of the appraisal.** Ask whether it came back "as is" or "subject to" repairs, and what exactly the repair condition says.
- **An electrician's quote**, if anything is flagged, so a seller repair or credit can be agreed while you still can.

## How we checked this

We searched three rulebooks word for word on 2026-11-26. The FHA one, Handbook 4000.1, runs to 1,871 pages (revised 08/13/2025); we looked for "fuse", "circuit breaker" and every mention of amps, then read the two pages that came up. Our copy came from the Internet Archive, since hud.gov blocks automated visitors with a bot check and we don't get around those. The VA chapter was searched the same way from an archived copy of the agency's file. Fannie Mae's sections B4-1.3-06, B7-3-01 and B7-3-02 came straight from Fannie Mae's own site, and the panel cost is our report engine's figure.

Left out: USDA loans (the USDA site refused our request), Freddie Mac, and any one insurer's underwriting rules. A USDA borrower, or anyone whose loan goes to Freddie Mac, should ask the loan officer which property rules apply.
