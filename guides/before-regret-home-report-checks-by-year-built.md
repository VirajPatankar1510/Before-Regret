# Before Regret Home Report: What It Checks by Year Built

[Before Regret](/) is a free home report for any US address. You type the address and the year the house was built, and it gives you four lists: what to inspect, what to ask the seller, which records to pull yourself, and what Census data shows about the neighborhood. Nothing in it is a finding about the house itself. The inspection list comes from 20 fixed rules keyed to the build year, the county and the state, and this page lists those rules exactly as the report engine holds them.

## What you type, and what gets looked up

You give the report three things: the address, the property type and the year built. The address is checked live against the Census Bureau's geocoder, which also places it in a census tract, and a few federal land datasets are checked to catch addresses that aren't homes at all.

The year built is taken on trust. The report says so in its own fine print: "The year built is the one you gave us; we haven't verified it." Since the year decides most of the list, getting it right matters more than anything else you type. If you're not sure of it, [finding when a house was built by address](/guides/when-was-my-house-built-by-address/) covers the records that hold it.

No AI writes a Before Regret report. Every line comes from the live lookups and the 20 fixed rules.

## The 20 checks and the build years each one covers

| Check | Shown for homes built | Where | Cost to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confirm you received the federal lead-paint disclosure | 1977 or earlier | Everywhere in the US | Free |
| Check for active knob-and-tube wiring | 1955 or earlier | Everywhere in the US | Usually included in a general inspection |
| Identify the branch circuit wiring material | 1965 to 1973 | Everywhere in the US | Usually included in a general inspection |
| Record the electrical panel brand | 1950 to 1989 | Everywhere in the US | Usually included in a general inspection |
| Scope the sewer line | 1973 or earlier | Everywhere in the US | $300 – $500 for a camera scope |
| Check the water supply pipe material | 1969 or earlier | Everywhere in the US | Usually included in a general inspection |
| Check whether the supply lines are polybutylene | 1978 to 1996 | Everywhere in the US | Usually included in a general inspection |
| Get the foundation looked at by a structural engineer | 1979 or earlier | Only in 13 counties with documented expansive clay soil | $400 – $800 for a structural engineer evaluation |
| Watch for signs of slab movement | 1980 or later | Only in 13 counties with documented expansive clay soil | Included in a general inspection; $400 – $800 for a structural engineer if signs are present |
| Have someone physically enter the crawlspace | 1965 or earlier | Only in 13 counties with documented expansive clay soil | Usually included in a general inspection when the crawlspace is accessible |
| Identify the foundation type and check for movement | Any year | Everywhere except the expansive-clay counties | Included in a general inspection |
| Test for asbestos only if you plan to renovate | 1985 or earlier | Everywhere in the US | $200 – $600 for sampling and lab analysis |
| If the exterior is stucco, find out which kind | 1990 to 2005 | Everywhere in the US | $400 – $800 for a moisture survey by an EIFS-qualified inspector |
| Get the age and remaining life of the roof in writing | 2014 or earlier (12+ years old) | Everywhere in the US | Included in a general inspection |
| Get the age of the HVAC system and water heater in writing | 2014 or earlier (12+ years old) | Everywhere in the US | Included in a general inspection |
| If the home has a fireplace or wood-burning stove, get a Level 2 chimney inspection | Any year | Everywhere in the US | $250 – $600 for a Level 2 inspection with a video flue scan |
| Get a wood-destroying insect (WDI) report | Any year | Only in 19 states (CA, AZ, NM, TX, OK, AR, LA, MS, AL, GA, FL, SC, NC, TN, KY, MO, KS, VA, WV) | $75 – $150 for a standalone WDI report |
| Consider a general pest and wood-destroying-insect inspection | Any year | Everywhere except the 19 termite-probability states | $75 – $150 for a standalone pest/WDI inspection |
| Check landslide susceptibility if the home is on a slope or hillside | Any year | Only in 15 states (WA, OR, CA, ID, MT, WY, CO, UT, PA, WV, VA, NC, TN, KY, AR) | Free |
| Radon test | Any year | Everywhere in the US | $150 – $300 |

Three of these also feed the report's insurance box: knob-and-tube wiring, the panel brand and polybutylene supply lines. That box never says what an insurer will do. For knob-and-tube it reads: "Ask your own carrier whether they will write a policy while it is still energized. Rules vary by carrier and state."

## Why a 1960s house gets the longest list

The Before Regret build-year windows overlap most in the middle of the last century. A house from 1955 sits inside the knob-and-tube window and the panel-brand window at once. A house from 1968 picks up aluminum branch wiring instead, along with cast iron sewer lines, galvanized supply pipe and the lead-paint disclosure.

We ran the engine for one county with no regional rules, Cook County, Illinois, and counted the items for a range of build years:

| Year built | Things to inspect | Marked check first |
|---|---|---|
| 1925 | 11 | 3 |
| 1955 | 12 | 4 |
| 1968 | 12 | 4 |
| 1985 | 9 | 2 |
| 2000 | 7 | 0 |
| 2015 | 4 | 0 |
| 2024 | 4 | 0 |

A newer house still gets a list. Radon, the foundation, pests and a fireplace apply to every build year, and once a house is 12+ years old the roof, the HVAC system and the water heater join them.

## Where the state or county changes the list

Three kinds of report check depend on location, and each one names a geology or climate claim that isn't true everywhere:

- **Expansive clay soil.** In 13 counties with documented expansive clay, the generic foundation item is swapped for three specific ones: a structural engineer for homes built 1979 or earlier, slab movement for 1980 or later, and a physical crawlspace entry for 1965 or earlier.
- **Termites.** In 19 states the report asks for a wood-destroying insect (WDI) report. Everywhere else a general pest inspection appears instead, at a lower priority.
- **Landslides.** In 15 states a hillside or slope check is added for any build year.

A report in South Pasadena, California, for example, gets the landslide item and the WDI report; the same 1925 house in Cook County gets neither.

## The seller questions and the records list

The seller questions come from the same year, county and state, plus the property type. Each one comes with a line on what a reassuring answer sounds like. The report is plain that it doesn't check what the seller says: "Answers are not checked by Before Regret; get anything important in writing."

The records list is the opposite of a finding. It names records the report did not look up, such as permits, the flood zone and code enforcement, and links to the office that holds each one. In the report's own words: "These 5 records were not checked for this address. Each links to the official source for this area, so you can look it up before you sign."

The neighborhood section is the only part pulled live for the address, and even it is about the area. Its figures (typical year built, owner costs, home values, commute) are labelled "Census figures for this neighborhood (census tract), not measurements of this house."

## What a Before Regret report can't tell you

A report can't tell you whether a given house has any of these problems. It tells you which ones are worth a look for a house of that age in that place, roughly what the check costs, and who to ask. An inspector, an electrician, a plumber with a camera or your own insurance agent gives you the answer.

The costs in the table are typical ranges for the check, not quotes. The sewer camera scope, for instance, is listed at $300 – $500; what a scope can turn up is in [what a sewer scope can reveal before you buy](/guides/sewer-scope-before-buying-house/).

## How we checked this

On 2026-11-24 we generated the table of all 20 rules with a script that reads the rules straight out of the report engine's source code, so nothing in it was retyped. The same script ran the engine for Cook County at seven build years to make the item counts above. The roof and HVAC window moves forward each year, because it is set at 12 years before the current year.

We also ran a live report on a public building's address, 1414 Mission Street in South Pasadena (City Hall), entered as a single-family home built in 1925, and read every section as a buyer would see it. It returned 12 things to inspect, 6 questions for the seller and 5 records to pull. We don't run private homes through the tool for articles.
