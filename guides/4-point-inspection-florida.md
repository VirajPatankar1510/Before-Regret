# Will Your House Pass a 4-Point Inspection in Florida?

A 4-point inspection is ordered for the insurance policy rather than for you, so it tends to come up when the agent starts quoting, not when you book the home inspector. The form itself is short and predictable: four pages of tick boxes and blanks, and almost everything that can hold up a policy sits on a handful of named lines. Knowing those lines before the inspector arrives turns the report from a surprise into a checklist you can act on during your inspection period.

## Who asks for a 4-point inspection in Florida

Citizens Property Insurance Corporation, the state-created insurer, [requires a four-point inspection for homes more than 20 years old](https://www.citizensfla.com/inspections) on property owner, dwelling and mobile home applications. Other insurers set their own age rules, so the agent quoting your policy is the one to ask.

Older housing is the norm in Florida, so this applies to a lot of houses. We pulled the Census Bureau's 2020-2024 American Community Survey for the state: of 10,256,470 homes, 67.7% were built in 1999 or earlier and 85.8% in 2009 or earlier, and the median year built is 1988. The survey groups the 2000s into one bin, which is why we can only give a range for "more than 20 years old".

Citizens publishes the 4-point form itself, Sample Form Insp4pt 03 25. Page 4 of Insp4pt 03 25 says the specific form is not required, but any other report must include at least the same level of detail, so whatever your inspector hands over will be checked against these boxes.

## The 4-point inspection form, section by section

The Florida 4-point inspection form spends one section on each system. This is what the inspector has to write down, and the line in each section that most often decides things.

| Section | What the inspector records | The line to watch | Our report engine's range if it needs fixing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electrical | Fuse or circuit breaker panel, total amps, panel age, year last updated, brand and model, wiring type; 12 named hazards plus an Other box | Cloth wiring, active knob and tube, and branch circuit aluminum wiring, each a box to tick | Panel replacement $1,500 – $4,000; aluminum wiring repair typically $2,000 – $8,000+; knob and tube rewire $8,000 – $30,000+ |
| Plumbing | Age of supply and drain piping (original, completely or partially re-piped), pipe types, water heater age and location, 10 fixtures rated Satisfactory or Unsatisfactory | Polybutylene, a separate Yes or No; active leak and prior leak, each a Yes or No | Whole-home repipe $4,000 – $15,000; water heater replacement $1,200 – $3,000 |
| HVAC | Central AC and central heat, age, year last updated, date of last servicing | Whether the system is in good working order; signs of leakage at the air handler, condensate line or drain pan | HVAC replacement $6,000 – $15,000 |
| Roof | Covering, age, remaining useful life, date of the last roofing permit, full or partial replacement and what percentage | Remaining useful life in years; 8 named damage signs; leaks in the attic or on interior ceilings | Full replacement $8,000 – $25,000+ |

The electrical hazard list is the longest on the 4-point form: double taps, blowing fuses, exposed wiring, tripping breakers, unsafe wiring, empty sockets, improper breaker size, loose wiring, scorching, improper grounding, corrosion and over fusing. If the house has a [Zinsco panel](/guides/will-zinsco-panel-fail-4-point-inspection/) or a [Pushmatic panel](/guides/pushmatic-panel-buying-house/), it is the brand and model line that names it.

Aluminum wiring has its own rule on the 4-point form. The form asks how the connections were repaired, with boxes for COPALUM crimps and AlumiConn connectors, and single-strand aluminum remediation needs separate paperwork certified by a licensed electrician. Our guide to [aluminum wiring repair costs](/guides/aluminum-wiring-repair-cost-before-closing/) covers which repair to ask a seller for.

The HVAC section also asks about a wood-burning stove or central gas fireplace and whether it was professionally installed, and whether a space heater is the main source of heat.

## The six photos the 4-point form requires

A Florida 4-point inspection form (Insp4pt 03 25) will not be accepted without photos, and the list is fixed: each side of the house, each slope of the roof, the water heater with the plumbing under the cabinets and any exposed valves, the main electrical panel open with its interior door label, the electrical box with the panel cover off, and every hazard or deficiency in the report.

Two of those photos tell the insurer more than the written lines do. The panel label photo shows the brand whatever was typed, and a picture of the box with the cover off shows the wiring itself. A seller can't describe their way past either one.

## Where the roof section meets Florida law

The 4-point roof section asks for a number in years, the remaining useful life, and Florida Statutes § 627.7011(5) turns that number into a test for roofs at least 15 years old. If an authorized inspector finds 5 years or more of useful life left, the insurer may not refuse to issue or renew solely because of the roof's age. For a roof under 15 years old, age alone is never a reason to refuse. The rule applies to policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2022.

Citizens sets its own line by material: a roof older than 25 years if it's shingle or similar, or 50 years if it's tile, slate, clay, concrete or metal, [needs documentation of at least five years of remaining useful life](https://www.citizensfla.com/inspections), and under five years means proof of a full replacement before a policy is written. With photos of every slope, the roof section of the 4-point can stand in for Citizens' separate Roof Inspection Form. Our [roof-age law guide](/guides/home-insurance-roof-age-law-by-state/) compares the Florida statute with five other states.

## What an Unsatisfactory box does to a purchase

An Unsatisfactory box on a 4-point form stops the insurance application, not just the report. Citizens' form says an agent [may not submit an application](https://www.citizensfla.com/documents/20702/48575/4-Point+Inspection+Form.pdf) for a house with electrical, heating or plumbing systems not in good working order or with existing hazards. The form defines acceptable condition as each system working as intended with no visible hazards or deficiencies.

The form is also not a verdict on the house. Page 1 says the information is used only to decide insurability and is not a warranty of how long anything will last, so a clean 4-point is not a substitute for a full home inspection. Our [home inspection guide](/guides/home-inspection-checklist-cost/) covers what a general inspection adds.

## Who can sign a 4-point form

A Florida-licensed professional has to complete, sign and date the 4-point form: a general, residential or building contractor, a building code inspector, or a home inspector. A trade professional can sign only their own section, so an electrician can sign the electrical section and nothing else. That matters after a repair: the electrician who fixed a double tap can document the electrical section again without a whole new inspection, if your insurer accepts that.

## Getting it done before your inspection period ends

- **Order the 4-point with your general inspection.** A Florida-licensed home inspector is one of the professionals the form accepts, so one visit can cover both, and the findings arrive while you can still negotiate.
- **Ask for the photos, not just the form.** You'll need them for the agent, and they are what a seller's electrician or roofer will want to see.
- **Send the form to your agent the day you get it.** Ask whether the policy can be written as the house stands, and which box, if any, is the problem.
- **Price the fix before you ask for it.** An electrician's or roofer's quote turns a ticked box into a repair request or a credit the seller can answer.
