# Can You Lose Home Insurance After Closing Over the Roof?

Sometimes, and the timing matters more than the roof. The riskiest stretch is the first weeks after the policy starts, when the insurer may still be checking the house. After that, state law narrows what an insurer can do mid-term, which leaves the renewal date as the other point where roof age can come up. The rules below are Texas's and Florida's, read at the [Texas Legislature](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/IN/htm/IN.551.htm#551.104) and the [Florida Senate](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/627.4133); your state's insurance department publishes its own.

## Cancellation and non-renewal are different letters

The Texas Department of Insurance defines cancellation as ending a policy "before the renewal date." Non-renewal is the insurer declining to continue it at the end of the term. A buyer who closes and then hears the roof is a problem needs to know which one they are holding, because the rules are not the same.

A binder is a third thing again. The same glossary calls it "a temporary insurance contract that provides proof of coverage until you receive a permanent policy." It is not the insurer's last word on the house.

## The first 60 days are when it can happen

**Texas.** Under Texas Insurance Code Section 551.104, an insurer may cancel a homeowners policy that has been in effect less than 60 days in two situations. One is when it identifies a condition that increases the risk, was not disclosed in the application, and was not the subject of a prior claim. The other is when, before the policy took effect, it did not accept a required inspection report from a licensed inspector, dated no earlier than 90 days before the policy started. A report the insurer does not reject before the 11th day after receiving it counts as accepted.

**Florida.** Under Florida Statutes § 627.4133, a cancellation during the first 60 days needs at least 20 days' written notice with the reason, unless there was a material misstatement or a failure to meet the insurer's underwriting requirements.

So the practical risk is a roof the application described one way and an inspection that shows another, or an underwriting requirement nobody met.

## After that, the reasons narrow

Once the policy has been in force for a while, both states limit cancellation to specific grounds.

In Texas, an insurer may cancel any policy for non-payment, a fraudulent claim, or when keeping it would break insurance law, and may cancel a non-auto policy for "an increase in the hazard covered by the policy that is within the control of the insured." The cancellation does not take effect until the 10th day after notice is mailed.

In Florida, after 60 days a residential policy may be cancelled only for a material misstatement, non-payment, a failure to meet within 60 days underwriting requirements set before coverage began, a substantial change in the risk, or a cancellation of a whole class of policyholders. Florida also requires at least 120 days' written notice of a cancellation or non-renewal, with the reason, except for non-payment.

## Roof age at renewal

When the issue is simply that the roof is getting old, the renewal date is where it can come up, and that comes with notice. In Texas, unless the insurer mails written notice of non-renewal at least 60 days before the policy expires, it must renew at your request. Whatever the letter, Texas Section 551.002 requires the insurer to state the precise reason and the source of the information it relied on.

Florida adds a roof-specific rule. Under Florida Statutes § 627.7011, for policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2022:

- An insurer may not refuse to issue or renew a policy solely because of roof age when the roof is less than 15 years old.
- For a roof at least 15 years old, it must let you have the roof inspected by an authorized inspector, at your expense, before requiring a replacement.
- If that inspection shows 5 years or more of useful life remaining, the insurer may not refuse to issue or renew solely because of roof age.

## If the insurer has the wrong roof date

Florida's law says a roof's age runs from the last date 100 percent of its surface was built or replaced. A partial repair does not reset the clock unless later work completes the whole surface, and a full replacement the insurer does not know about can make a newer roof look old on paper.

The fix is paperwork: the contractor's invoice, the permit and its final inspection, and the date on each. Here is [how to prove your roof's age for insurance](/guides/prove-roof-age-for-insurance/), and if the date came from an aerial image rather than a visit, this guide explains [why roofs get flagged from aerial photos](/guides/home-insurance-aerial-photos-roof/). In Texas, the written reason the insurer must give you will also name the source it relied on, which tells you what to correct.

## If coverage lapses while you have a mortgage

If your mortgage contract requires hazard insurance, the federal mortgage servicing rule, 12 CFR 1024.37, governs what happens if it lapses. A servicer may charge you for force-placed insurance only if it has a reasonable basis to believe you have not kept the required coverage, and only after sending a written notice at least 45 days before charging and a reminder at least 15 days before. If you then show it you had coverage in place all along, it must cancel the force-placed policy within 15 days and refund the overlapping charges.

If you get a cancellation or non-renewal notice, call your insurer and your mortgage servicer the same week, not the week coverage ends.

## Before you close: get these answers in writing

- **Is anything still pending?** Ask whether an inspection or any underwriting requirement is outstanding, and its deadline.
- **What roof date do you have on file?** Compare it with the seller's invoice and permit before the policy starts, not after.
- **Was the inspection report accepted?** In Texas, a report not rejected within the window counts as accepted; ask anyway, and keep the answer.
- **Is the roof a renewal condition?** Ask whether its age or condition will be reviewed at the first renewal, and what would satisfy the insurer.
- **When will the permanent policy replace the binder?** When it arrives, check that its declarations page matches what the binder showed.

If the answers point to a replacement, that is a negotiation to have with the seller before your inspection deadline. This guide covers [how to tell how old a roof is](/guides/how-to-tell-how-old-a-roof-is/) before you make an offer.
