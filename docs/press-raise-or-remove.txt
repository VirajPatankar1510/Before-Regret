THE FOUR NUMBERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

  32,779   FEMA-funded decisions on single-family homes, fiscal 1996-2025,
           where the choice was buy-and-demolish or raise-in-place

  24,020   were demolished.  8,759 were raised.  A ratio of 2.7 to 1

  5 of 25  states do nearly all the elevating. Florida, Louisiana, New
           Jersey, Virginia and Washington demolish 31.1% of the time.

   48.9    percentage-point spread between foundation types in those five

           with basements. In the other twenty the same spread is 22.7 points
           against a base rate of 90.7%, which is to say it barely matters.

The line that carries the whole study: whether a flooded house is raised or
removed is decided first by the state, and only then by what it stands on.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE THING TO CHECK BEFORE YOU WRITE IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

The obvious version of this story is "houses with basements get demolished" -
93.4% of them nationally, against 46.3% of homes on piers. We nearly published
that and it does not survive a check.

Louisiana has pier-built housing AND raises houses. Iowa has basements AND
demolishes them. State could explain the entire effect. Asking the question
inside states instead of across them is what produces the real finding, which
is that the foundation matters enormously in five states and almost not at all
in the other twenty.

If you use the national basement figure without that caveat, you are reporting
a confound. The study says so in its own limitations, and we would rather you
heard it from us.

PITCH 1 - CLIMATE OR ENVIRONMENT DESK
The angle: managed retreat is not a national policy, it is five states
==========================================================================

SUBJECT:
The US demolishes 2.7 flooded homes for every one it raises - and five states do all the raising

Dear [Name],

I have finished an analysis of FEMA's Hazard Mitigation Assistance property
file - every home the agency has paid to buy, demolish or elevate - and the
national picture is more lopsided than the managed-retreat debate suggests.

Across 32,779 single-family decisions between fiscal 1996 and 2025, 24,020
homes were bought and demolished and 8,759 were raised in place. Demolition
wins 2.7 to 1.

The distribution is the story. Ranked by how often they demolish, the 25 states
with meaningful volume do not form a spectrum - they form two groups with an
empty gap between them. Florida, Louisiana, New Jersey, Virginia and Washington
sit between 22.6% and 51.2%. Then nothing until North Carolina at 71.5%, after
which twenty states run to 100%. Kansas and Oklahoma have never raised a
single-family home with these grants.

So "should America retreat or adapt" is not really a live national question.
Twenty states have already answered it one way and five the other, and the line
between them is not gradual.

PITCH 2 - DATA OR INVESTIGATIONS DESK
The angle: the confound, and what survives it
==========================================================================

SUBJECT:
A dataset of 32,779 federal decisions on flooded homes - and the finding that nearly wasn't

Dear [Name],

Something for a data desk, including the part that failed.

FEMA publishes every property its mitigation grants have paid to alter or
remove - 99,927 records back to 1989, with county, ZIP, structure type and
foundation type on each. Filtered to single-family homes facing the
buy-or-raise choice, that is 32,779 decisions.

Sorted by foundation, the answer looks obvious: 93.4% of homes with basements
were demolished, against 46.3% of homes already on piers. Clean engineering
gradient.

It does not hold. Louisiana has pier-built housing and raises houses; Iowa has
basements and demolishes them, so state could account for all of it. Asking
within states splits the country: in the five states that use elevation at all,
foundation moves the outcome by 48.9 points. In the other twenty it moves it by
22.7 against a base rate of 90.7% - everything goes regardless of how it was
built.

The conditional version is the finding. I am sending it with the disconfirmation
attached because that is the part a data desk will want to check, and the state
and ZIP tables are published so you can run it yourself.

PITCH 3 - HOUSING, REAL ESTATE OR PERSONAL FINANCE DESK
The angle: what it means for someone buying a house
==========================================================================

SUBJECT:
What happens to a flooded house depends on your state first, your foundation second

Dear [Name],

A finding from federal data that has a practical edge for buyers, which is
unusual for this subject.

Across 32,779 FEMA-funded decisions on flooded single-family homes since 1996,
73.3% ended with the house bought and demolished rather than raised. But that
national figure hides two different countries. Five states - Florida,
Louisiana, New Jersey, Virginia and Washington - demolish 31.1% of the time.
The other twenty demolish 90.7% of the time.

And in those five states, the foundation under the house changes the outcome
more than anything else measured: 17.8% of homes on piers or piles were
removed, against 66.7% of homes with basements. In the twenty that rarely
elevate, that distinction nearly vanishes.

For a buyer looking at a house near water, the order matters. Which state you
are in sets whether saving the house is on the table at all. What it stands on
decides the rest - and foundation type is something a home inspection tells you
before you commit.

There is also a ZIP lookup on the page covering the 1,461 ZIP codes with at
least five decisions, so a reader can check their own area.

IF A REPORTER COMES BACK
==========================================================================

"Is this every buyout in America?"
  No, and the study leads with that. These are FEMA-funded decisions only.
  States, counties and cities run their own buyout programmes and homeowners
  elevate privately; none of that is in the file. A ZIP with no record is not a
  ZIP where nothing happened.

"Is it a sample of flooded homes?"
  No. A property is here because somebody applied for a grant and the
  application succeeded. Every figure describes funded decisions, not houses at
  risk, and the direction of that selection bias is unknown.

"Did FEMA choose to demolish basement homes, or was raising them impossible?"
  The data cannot separate preference from engineering constraint, and the
  study says so. Lifting a house over a basement may simply be impractical. Do
  not let anyone quote us as saying a state preferred to demolish a particular
  house.

"What did it cost?"
  Unknown from this file. A payment amount is present on only 47.8% of the
  relevant rows, which is why no cost-per-decision figure appears anywhere in
  the study. Anyone publishing an average cost from this dataset is estimating.

"Hasn't this been done?"
  Partly, and say so first. The Natural Resources Defense Council and Climate
  Central have both published analyses of FEMA acquisitions. What appears to be
  new here is the cut by foundation type and the two-regime state split.

"Are you selling something?"
  Yes - BeforeRegret sells a property report to home buyers. The study is free,
  the data is public domain, and the analysis does not depend on the product.
