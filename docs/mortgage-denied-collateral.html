Denied Because of the House

Most mortgage denials are about the buyer: debt, credit, cash. Some are about the house. Lenders must report why they turn down a home loan, and in 2024 they named the property itself, the collateral, in 83,247 denials of home-purchase applications. We counted them by loan type, home type, neighbourhood housing age, state and county.

Key findings

  • Of 4,319,109 home-purchase mortgage applications that lenders approved or denied in 2024, 623,942 were denied, and 83,247 of those denials named the collateral, the property itself, as a reason (HMDA 2024).
  • For a first-lien loan on a single-unit home the buyer would live in, collateral was a reason in 10.7% of denials that gave reasons, or 15.3 per 1,000 applications decided.
  • For site-built homes in census tracts where the median home was 40 to 59 years old, collateral denials ran at 19.2 per 1,000 applications, against 9.3 in tracts where it was under 20 years, while overall denial rates were within 1 percentage point of each other.
  • For manufactured homes, collateral was a reason in 32.8% of FHA denials and 39.8% of VA denials that gave reasons, against 2.8% for conventional loans.
  • Among counties with at least 3,000 applications from buyers who would live in the home, New York County (Manhattan), New York had the highest rate of collateral denials, 86.0 per 1,000 applications.

Source: FFIEC Home Mortgage Disclosure Act (HMDA) data, 2024 nationwide loan/application records, home purchase, action taken originated, approved but not accepted or denied; pulled 2026-11-25. Analysis by Before Regret.

What “collateral” means on a denial

The lender’s reason, not the full story.

Lenders covered by the Home Mortgage Disclosure Act report every home-loan application they receive. Regulation C, 12 CFR 1003.4(a)(16), requires them to report, for a denied application, “the principal reason or reasons the financial institution denied the application”, up to four. The FFIEC’s field definitions give nine codes, and code 4 is simply “Collateral”.

Collateral on a home loan is the home. Regulation B’s sample adverse-action notice words the same reason “Value or type of collateral not sufficient”. A denial coded this way can follow an appraisal below the price, a house in a condition the loan program won’t accept, or a type of property the lender won’t lend on. HMDA doesn’t say which, so neither does this page.

How often the house is the reason

About 1 in 10 denials for a typical buyer.

For the typical buyer, a first-lien loan on a single-unit home they would live in, lenders decided 3,580,613 home-purchase applications in 2024 and denied 517,470, 14.5%. Collateral was a reason in 54,934 of those denials: 10.7% of the ones that gave reasons, or 15.3 for every 1,000 applications decided. In 90% of them, the lender listed collateral as the first reason.

The buyer’s own finances come up far more. Debt-to-income ratio was a reason in 40.0% of denials and credit history in 36.2%. A lender can give more than one reason, so the shares add to more than 100%.

Older neighbourhoods

Twice the rate where the typical home is 40 to 59 years old.

HMDA attaches census data to every application, including the median age of the homes in its census tract. For site-built homes in 2024, collateral denials ran at 19.2 per 1,000 applications in tracts where the median home was 40 to 59 years old and 18.4 where it was 60 or more, against 9.3 in tracts where it was under 20.

The overall denial rate hardly moved across those tracts, staying between 8.5% and 9.3%. What changed was the reason: in older neighbourhoods more of the denials were about the property. The same pattern holds for conventional and FHA loans taken separately. The tract median is a neighbourhood figure; a new house can sit in an old tract.

Manufactured homes

For FHA and VA, the house is often the problem.

Manufactured homes are a different market. Lenders denied 58.3% of 403,254 manufactured-home purchase applications from buyers who would live in them, mostly for credit history. Collateral rarely comes up on conventional manufactured-home loans: 2.8% of denials. On government-backed ones it’s far more common. Collateral was a reason in 32.8% of FHA denials and 39.8% of VA denials for manufactured homes, or 50.2 and 55.0 per 1,000 applications, against 14.0 and 8.3 for site-built homes with the same loans.

Second homes, rentals and multi-unit buildings

More than twice the rate.

Collateral denials were more than twice as common for homes the buyer wouldn’t live in, and for small multi-unit buildings. For a single-unit investment property, 35.0 applications in 1,000 were denied with collateral as a reason, and collateral was named in 28.4% of denials that gave reasons. For a 2-to-4-unit building the buyer would live in, it was 43.2 per 1,000.

By county

Five big urban counties stand out.

Among the 297 counties with at least 3,000 applications from typical buyers, New York County (Manhattan), New York had the highest rate of collateral denials, 86.0 per 1,000 applications, and collateral was a reason in 57.8% of its denials that gave reasons. What this means if you’re buying

Two rights to use if the house is the reason.

A collateral denial is uncommon for a typical buyer, but it comes late, after the appraisal or the lender’s review of the property. Federal rules give you two things to work with.

  • A copy of the appraisal. Regulation B, 12 CFR 1002.14, requires the lender to give you a copy of every appraisal and written valuation developed for a first-lien loan on a home, promptly after it’s done or three business days before closing, whichever is earlier. The rule attaches to the application, whether or not the loan closes.
  • The reason in writing. Regulation B, 12 CFR 1002.9, requires a notice within 30 days of a completed application being denied, and the statement of reasons “must be specific and indicate the principal reason(s)”. If the reason is the collateral, ask which part: the value, the condition, or the type of property.

Before an offer, the pattern in this data points at a few questions worth asking about an older house, a manufactured home on an FHA or VA loan, or a two-to-four-unit building: what condition the main systems are in, whether past work had permits, and whether the loan program you plan to use has rules for this kind of property. Method

How the figures were built.

The data is the FFIEC’s 2024 nationwide HMDA loan/application records, filtered in the HMDA data browser to home-purchase loans with action taken “loan originated”, “application approved but not accepted” or “application denied”. We streamed the full file on 2026-11-25 and counted it without keeping any rows: 4,319,109 records, matching the data browser’s own count, with no malformed rows. Withdrawn and incomplete files are left out, so “applications” means applications a lender decided.

A denial counts as citing collateral if collateral is any of its up-to-four reasons. Shares are of denials that gave reasons: lenders with a partial exemption may report reason code 1111, exempt, and those 9,122 denials are left out of the denominator. “Typical buyer” means a first-lien loan on a single-unit home the buyer would live in as a principal residence. Regulation C counts the dwelling units securing the loan, so a loan on one condominium or co-op unit is a single unit. County names come from the Census Bureau’s 2023 county list.

Limitations

What this does not show.

Not every lender reports. Regulation C, 12 CFR 1003.2(g), covers lenders above size and volume thresholds, including a minimum of 25 closed-end mortgage loans in each of the two prior years, and a lender must have had an office in a metropolitan area. Small lenders and some rural ones aren’t in the data.

Applications, not buyers or houses. A buyer turned down by one lender may apply to another, and each application counts. A denied application doesn’t mean the house didn’t sell.

The reason is the lender’s. HMDA records what the lender reported, in its own categories. It doesn’t record the appraised value, the condition of the house, or why the collateral fell short. Housing age is the tract median from census data, not the age of the house. 39,818 typical-buyer applications have no state recorded and 51,477 have no county we could name; they are in the national figures only.

Questions this data answers

What does it mean when a mortgage is denied for collateral?

It means the lender reported the property itself as a reason it would not make the loan. Lenders report it under HMDA as denial reason "Collateral", and Regulation B's sample notice words it "Value or type of collateral not sufficient". The data does not say which: a low appraisal, the condition of the house, or a type of property the loan program will not accept.

How often are mortgages denied because of the appraisal or the property?

In 2024, 83,247 of 623,942 denied home-purchase applications named collateral as a reason. For a first-lien loan on a single-unit home the buyer would live in, it was a reason in 10.7% of denials that gave reasons, or 15.3 per 1,000 applications decided. Debt-to-income ratio and credit history were cited far more often.

Are mortgages on older homes denied more often because of the house?

In 2024 HMDA data, yes by neighbourhood: for site-built homes, collateral denials ran at 19.2 per 1,000 applications in census tracts where the median home was 40 to 59 years old, against 9.3 where it was under 20. The overall denial rate barely changed. HMDA records the tract's median housing age, not the age of the house being bought.

Can I get a copy of the appraisal if my mortgage is denied?

Yes. Regulation B (12 CFR 1002.14) requires a lender to give you a copy of every appraisal and written valuation developed for a first-lien loan on a home, promptly after it is completed or three business days before closing, whichever is earlier, even if the loan does not close. If credit is denied, Regulation B (12 CFR 1002.9) also requires a notice within 30 days, and the statement of reasons must be specific.

Where can I download mortgage denial data by county?

This study publishes two CSV files under a CC BY 4.0 licence: every county and every state, with 2024 home-purchase applications, denials and the count of denials citing each reason, for first-lien loans on single-unit homes the buyer would live in. The underlying data is the public FFIEC HMDA data browser, which needs no key.
