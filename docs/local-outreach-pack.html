Before you send

Four things that keep this honest if a reporter pushes back.

  • The zone is the rated zone on the policy, not today’s map. Claims span the 1970s to 2026 and maps have been redrawn many times. Never let this be printed as “X% of homes are outside today’s flood zone.”
  • NFIP only. Private flood cover is not counted.
  • It is descriptive. It does not explain why — map vintage, drainage, development and enforcement all plausibly contribute.
  • If asked how much bigger the real gap is: say you don’t know. People who buy flood cover unprompted are not a random sample, so the numbers cannot simply be scaled up.

Subject

More than half of Harris County's flood insurance claims were paid outside the flood zone

Body

Hi Jonathan,
I've finished an analysis of every flood insurance claim FEMA has ever paid, and the Harris County number seems worth your desk's time.
Of the 167,261 NFIP claims on record in Harris County, 54.8% were paid on homes that were rated outside the high-risk flood zone - the zone that triggers the insurance requirement when you take a mortgage. That's about $4.1 billion paid to people the map said were not the ones at risk.
Statewide it's 50.7% of 380,408 Texas claims. Nationally it's 26.6%. Texas is roughly double the national rate, and Harris is the single biggest piece of it.
The part I'd point a reporter at: outside those mapped zones, only 1.25% of homes carry any flood insurance at all, against 47.9% inside. So all of that $4.1 billion came from the small minority who bought a policy nobody made them buy. Everyone else who flooded outside the zone simply isn't in the data.
Two things I'd want stated in any coverage. The zone recorded is the one the policy was rated in at the time, not today's map, and these claims go back to the 1970s. And this counts NFIP policies only, not private flood insurance.
