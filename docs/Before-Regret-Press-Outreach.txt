Three quarters of modelled US building loss falls outside the standard homeowners policy

Dear Andrea,

I've published a county-level analysis of homeowners insurance, and one finding may be more useful to your readers than the pricing result it started as.

Using FEMA's National Risk Index and separating perils by whether a standard HO-3 responds to them, 76.5% of the expected annual building loss to US homes comes from perils the policy excludes. Inland flooding alone accounts for 59.2% of it; earthquake a further 16.1%.

The exposure concentrates in older housing. In the 158 counties FEMA scores at or above 95 for earthquake risk, 22,030,850 homes — 57% of the housing stock there — were built before 1980, predating the substantially strengthened seismic provisions of the 1976 Uniform Building Code.

For context on the pricing side: across 3,093 counties, hazard risk explains 20% of the variation in what households report paying, while state of residence alone explains 44%.

WHY NO NAMES HERE. Every address in this pack was copied from Insurance

WHO THIS IS FOR: infrastructure, public-safety and state-government
reporters, and local desks in a state with a lot of these dams. It is a
stronger local story than a national one.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
GET THE NUMBER RIGHT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

There are three separate figures here and they are easy to blur into one.
Do not.

  17,049   dams classified high hazard potential (of 92,766 in the inventory)
   2,791   of those rated Poor or Unsatisfactory        - 16.4%
   2,476   of those with NO emergency action plan       - 14.5%
     636   in BOTH groups: poor or unsatisfactory AND no plan on file

636 is the intersection. It is the number to lead with, and it is the one
that gets miscopied as "over 2,000 dams in poor condition with no emergency
plan" - which is 2,476 and 2,791 mashed together and describes neither group.
If a reporter writes that back to you, correct it before it runs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUBJECT:
636 US dams are rated poor or unsatisfactory and have no emergency action plan on file
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Dear [NAME],

I have pulled a county-level read of the US Army Corps of Engineers National
Inventory of Dams that may be useful to you, and one figure in it is the
story.

The inventory holds 92,766 dams. 17,049 carry a hazard classification of
High, meaning failure is expected to cause loss of human life - a statement
about consequences, not about condition. Of those 17,049:

  - 2,791 (16.4%) have a recorded condition of Poor or Unsatisfactory
  - 2,476 (14.5%) have no emergency action plan on file
  - 636 are in both groups

An emergency action plan is a procedural document: who is notified, in what
order, and usually the inundation mapping showing who is downstream. Condition
and plan status are separate fields in the inventory, which is what makes the
overlap worth reporting rather than assuming.

Four things I would want stated in any coverage, because they cut against the
alarming reading:

  1. A further 3,784 high-hazard dams (22.2%) have NO condition rating
     recorded at all. A missing rating is not a good rating, and it is not a
     bad one either - it is missing.
  2. 77.4% of high-hazard dams (13,201) DO have an emergency action plan.
  3. The median high-hazard dam has a last inspection date of 2023, and among
     those rated Poor or Unsatisfactory the median assessment date is also
     2023. These records exist because an active inspection regime produced
     them. The story is what the file shows, not that nobody is looking.
  4. The inventory does not publish the reasoning behind any condition
     assessment, and age does not determine condition - the same file holds
     dams of similar vintage rated Satisfactory.

The county table covers 2,274 counties with at least one high-hazard dam, so
there is a local cut for almost any newsroom. One note if you total it: the
county file comes to 16,931 rather than 17,049, because 37 dams are not
placed in a state and 81 carry no county. The 636 appears as 635 there for
the same reason. Every national figure above counts all 17,049.
