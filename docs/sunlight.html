Which direction should a house face?

The honest answer is that it depends which room you mean. “South facing” describes a front door. The bedroom behind it might face north and never see a sunrise all year. What a south facing house actually means

Almost everything written about a south facing house is describing the street elevation — which way the front of the building points. That tells you about the front rooms and nothing at all about the back ones. A south facing house has north-facing rooms in it, and those rooms behave exactly like rooms in a north facing house.

South-facing windows do get the most sun overall, and they get it through the middle of the day rather than at either end. In winter that is the warmest room in the house. In an un-shaded August it is the one nobody wants to sit in.

A north facing house is not the bad one

This is the result that surprises people. In midsummer the sun rises well north of due east — at 40°N it clears the horizon at a bearing of about 58° in June, against about 121° in December. So a north-facing window gets a genuine run of early light for several months, then loses it entirely for the winter. Set the direction to north above and the table shows the split.

What a north facing room really lacks is not light but direct light. It gets steady, even daylight all day and very little glare, which is why north-facing rooms have been the traditional choice for studios and desks.

East facing and west facing windows

These two differ most, and the difference is the time of day. An east facing house takes the sun early and loses it around midday. A west facing house is the mirror image: nothing in the morning, then everything from mid-afternoon until sunset, along with the heat that comes with it in summer.

Neither is better in the abstract. East light suits a bedroom or a kitchen you use in the morning. West light suits a room you sit in after work, if you can shade it in July.

What blocks the light

A compass direction assumes an open sky, and most houses do not have one. The neighbouring roofline, a mature oak, a ridge behind the property — any of these routinely matter more than the direction does. A two-storey house about fifteen feet away blocks roughly the first 15° of sky, which on a midwinter morning is the difference between first light at 7:19 and first light at 9:07.

That is not a rounding error. In winter an obstruction does not reduce a room’s morning light, it removes it. Switch the second setting above to compare the same window both ways — for many houses this is the only comparison that matters.

Where the numbers come from

Sun position is worked out for every minute of the day using the standard NOAA solar position algorithm, corrected for atmospheric refraction near the horizon. A window receives direct sun when the sun is above the horizon and within 90° of the way the window faces. County coordinates are the internal points published in the US Census Bureau 2023 Gazetteer.

What this doesn’t tell you

It tells you whether direct sun reaches the glass, not how bright the room will feel — window size, glazing, wall colour and indirect daylight all matter and none of them are here. The obstruction setting is one approximation, not a model of your neighbour’s actual roofline. And it counts minutes of sunlight; it makes no claim about health or sleep.
