The flood-zone line in Florida’s newest disaster counties

FEMA declared a major disaster for severe storms and flooding in seven Florida counties on 24 November 2026: Brevard, Hardee, Indian River, Martin, Okeechobee, Palm Beach and St. Lucie. We went back through the flood-insurance and disaster-aid records we already hold for those same counties. In Brevard, 61.0% of past flood claims came from homes outside the mapped high-risk zone: more than three times the Florida share, and the highest of any Florida county with enough claims to compare.

Key findings

  • In Brevard County, Florida, 61.0% of the 5,667 National Flood Insurance Program claims on record were on homes rated outside FEMA’s mapped high-risk flood zone, against 14.8% for Florida as a whole (NFIP claims to 1 June 2026).
  • Brevard’s 61.0% is the highest out-of-zone share of the 41 Florida counties with at least 500 NFIP claims on record, and all 5 DR-4956-FL counties with that many claims are above Florida’s 14.8%.
  • Inside the mapped high-risk zone, NFIP take-up in the seven DR-4956 counties runs from 14.9% of homes in Hardee County to 77.8% in Martin County.
  • In past major disasters, FEMA approved 39% of 604,468 homeowner registrations from these seven counties and paid $1,830 on average per approved registration; the housing-assistance maximum for a disaster declared on or after 1 October 2026 is $46,400.

Sources: FEMA OpenFEMA NFIP claims and residential penetration rates; FEMA Housing Assistance (Owners) and Disaster Declarations Summaries, read 27 November 2026; analysis by Before Regret. Figures describe past claims and past disasters, not the 31 October 2026 storms.

The declaration

Seven counties, and help for households in 6 of them.

FEMA’s declaration DR-4956-FL, for severe storms and flooding that began on 31 October 2026, names Brevard, Hardee, Indian River, Martin, Okeechobee, Palm Beach and St. Lucie counties. FEMA’s own declaration record shows its Individuals and Households Program, the aid a homeowner applies for, declared in Brevard, Indian River, Martin, Okeechobee, Palm Beach and St. Lucie. Hardee County is listed for Public Assistance, the program that pays local governments, and not for household aid. Designations can be added after a declaration, so FEMA’s page for DR-4956 is the current word on who can apply.

Flood insurance claims outside the flood zone

In Brevard, most flood claims were on homes the map placed outside the high-risk zone.

The National Flood Insurance Program records the flood zone each policy was rated in. Zones starting with A or V are FEMA’s Special Flood Hazard Area, the mapped high-risk zone where a federally backed mortgage requires flood insurance. Zones X, B and C sit outside it. In Florida as a whole, 14.8% of 444,150 classifiable claims were outside the mapped zone. In all 5 declared counties with enough claims to compare, the share is higher.

Brevard’s figure is the highest of the 41 Florida counties with enough claims to rank, and Palm Beach’s is the 5th highest. Across all seven counties, claims on homes rated outside the mapped zone were paid $123.3 million, out of 26,382 claims on record.

A high share does not mean the map is wrong about any one street. It means that, in these counties, a “not in a flood zone” answer has not kept flood claims away in the past. Flood zones are also redrawn, and a claim is counted by the zone its policy was rated in at the time. Our Outside the Zone study has the same measure for 1,921 US counties.

What FEMA has paid here before

Disaster aid in these counties has averaged $1,830 per approved homeowner.

FEMA’s Housing Assistance data for owners covers every major disaster declared from 2002. Across the seven counties, 604,468 homeowner registrations were made and 39% were approved for any aid. Approved owners received $1,830 on average, in nominal dollars. FEMA aid is not insurance: by regulation it does not pay for losses insurance covers (44 CFR 206.113), and repair aid restores a home to a safe and sanitary condition, not to how it was (44 CFR 206.117).

The ceiling for this declaration is $46,400, the maximum set for disasters declared on or after 1 October 2026 in FEMA’s annual adjustment, published in the Federal Register.

If you are buying in one of these counties

Questions the records above make worth asking.

  • Ask the seller about past flood claims and whether the house took water in the storms that began on 31 October 2026.
  • Price flood insurance even outside a high-risk flood zone. In Brevard, Palm Beach, Martin and Indian River, 39.6% or more of past claims came from homes the map placed outside it.
  • Check the permit record for past repairs. If the house was repaired after a flood, the permit record shows what was filed. Our guides for Brevard County permits and Martin County permits show where to look.

How we checked this

On 27 November 2026 we read the declaration for DR-4956 from FEMA’s Disaster Declarations Summaries API: its counties, its incident start date and which programs each county was designated for. The county figures come from three analyses we had already built from OpenFEMA data: NFIP claims to 1 June 2026 (Outside the Zone), NFIP residential penetration rates as of 3 August 2026 (Risk Without Cover) and Housing Assistance for owners (What FEMA Pays Homeowners). This page adds nothing to those datasets; it reads the seven declared counties out of them and compares them with Florida.

What this page does not know: anything about the 31 October 2026 storms themselves, how many homes flooded, or what any one house’s risk is. These are county records, not findings about a particular property.
