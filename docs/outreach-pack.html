Before you send

Four rules that keep this honest

  1. State the data vintage every time. ACS 5-year 2023, centred near 2021. If a reporter publishes these as current premiums, that is a correction with your name on it. Every pitch below says so in the body.
  2. Do not claim causation. The study shows state explains more variation than risk. It does not show why, and says so. If asked, the honest answer is “we can’t separate rate regulation from reinsurance costs from litigation environment.”
  3. Send from a real mailbox. hello@beforeregret.com is the only address that exists. Do not invent a press alias.
  4. One outlet at a time, no BCC blasts. Trade editors bin mass mail, and Insurance Journal’s editors are beat-assigned — sending the Florida angle to the West editor marks you as someone who did not read their page.

Subject: County-level dataset: reported home insurance premiums vs FEMA modelled loss Dear [Dr. name], I've assembled a county-level dataset that may be useful to your work on [their topic], and I'd value your view on whether the central result is already known. It pairs ACS table B25141 - reported homeowners insurance costs for mortgaged households - with per-peril expected annual building loss from FEMA's National Risk Index, across 3,093 counties. Regressing log premium on log hazard risk (restricted to perils a standard HO-3 covers) gives R^2 = 0.196. State fixed effects alone give 0.436. Within-state, the median correlation between risk and premium is +0.218, and in 11 of 43 states it is negative. I want to be straightforward about the limits: this is descriptive, there is no identification strategy, and I have not done a formal literature review. Sequencing

What to do this week

  1. Day 1 — Insurance Journal national. One email to Chad Hemenway. Wait 3 working days before trying Andrea Wells; they share a desk and double-sending looks careless.
  2. Day 1 — the three regional editors. Rabb, Amacher, Jergler. These are genuinely different stories, so sending all three the same day is fine and not spammy.
  3. Day 2 — Carrier Management. Susanne Sclafane. More analytical readership; the 76.5% exclusion figure leads better than the ranking there.
  4. Days 3–5 — personal finance. Look up four bylines, send four notes. This tier takes the most research and has the highest authority payoff.
  5. Ongoing — source platforms. Connectively, Qwoted, SourceBottle. Answer housing and insurance queries as a named person. Highest-frequency channel you have, and it compounds.

Realistic expectation

Cold pitching converts at low single digits. Sending eleven well-targeted emails might yield one or two responses, and that would be a good outcome from a standing start of zero backlinks. The regional angle has the best odds; the personal-finance tier has the highest value per placement. Neither pays off in a week.
