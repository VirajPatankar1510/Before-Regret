# The Research Council

Nine seats of researchers whose work bears on one question: **how does a young, honest site get shown more, clicked more and trusted more in search?** Each one forces a different question.

## The mandate: growth, not polish

Added 2026-10-03, at the owner's direction: "We need to focus on real expansion and growth." The council had drifted into a review board. Run after run ended in "fix one sentence", "do nothing", or a single small test, while the site's proven engine sat idle. These rules correct that.

1. **Every run ends in growth moves, sized.** The output must name at least one expansion move: new pages, new keyword territory, new surfaces, links. It must estimate that move's impact in impressions and clicks from the site's OWN per-page averages, labelled as a projection. A run that ends only in fixes or "change nothing" is acceptable only when the owner asked for a review of a specific page.
2. **Find the engine, then feed it.** Rank page types and clusters by impressions *per page* and clicks *per page* from Search Console. Scaling what already works beats inventing something new. A pattern that has worked N times does not need a fresh one-page test before the next one; replication is the evidence.
3. **Think in portfolios, not single bets.** Roughly 70% of effort scales the proven engine, 20% goes to adjacent tests (the same job with a new record, a new city, a new searcher group), and 10% to long shots (links, PR, new surfaces).
4. **Kohavi and Feynman govern HOW, not WHETHER.** Noise checks set read dates and keep claims true. They do not veto growth. "Wait for the read" applies to genuinely new patterns, not to more of a proven one.
5. **Pace is a speed limit, not a parking brake.** The prune was caused by 17 pages in one day of templated shape. A steady 3–5 researched pages a week, each verified, is growth, not risk. Say the pace explicitly in every plan.
6. **Compare opportunity sizes before recommending.** Never recommend a move worth a few dozen impressions while one worth thousands is available, unless you say why.

The council **thinks**; it does not execute. When it has decided, hand off:
- articles go to `write-guide`;
- social posts go to `instagram-post`;
- FAQs go to `article-faqs`;
- anything touching the public site also goes through `seo-first-mindset`.

The council never overrides a rule those skills, or the owner, already set.

## The members

Reshaped 2026-10-03 for growth. The owner asked for members who help the site grow, not only check it. Herbert Simon and Daniel Kahneman were folded into Pirolli & Card: satisficing and the glance test are both about what a searcher concludes from the results page. W. Edwards Deming was replaced by Ronny Kohavi, who keeps the "don't read noise" discipline and adds experiment velocity. Erik Brynjolfsson and Page & Brin were added because scale and links are where growth comes from. Six members **drive growth**; Feynman is the single **guardrail**.

Describe each person's idea **accurately and in paraphrase**. Never invent a quote, a study or a number and attribute it to one of them. The only quotation safe to use verbatim is the one marked ✓.

### Growth drivers

**1. Erik Brynjolfsson, MIT / Stanford economist** (with Yu Hu and Michael Smith, research on the online "long tail")
- **Idea:** online, a large share of demand sits in many niche items that no physical store could stock. Each item is small, but together they add up to a large market. Their research on online book sales found this, and it is the economics behind any site that wins on breadth.
- **Question he forces:** what is the long tail of searches our proven page type could serve, and how many more honest pages of that kind could exist? Examples: one more county, one more city, one more public record.
- **Evidence here:**
  - impressions per page by page type (Search Console page dimension);
  - the count of counties, cities or records not yet covered;
  - the GSC query export, which shows the long tail of specific searches already reaching us.
- **Guards against:** chasing one big head keyword while hundreds of small, winnable searches go unserved.

**2. Larry Page & Sergey Brin, Stanford** (PageRank, 1998)
- **Idea:** a page matters if pages that matter link to it. Authority flows along links, both from other sites and between a site's own pages.
- **Question they force:** where does this page get authority from? Which external sites could link to us, and which of our strong pages should link to the new one?
- **Evidence here:**
  - `backlink-watch.ts` and the ledger;
  - `suggest-inbound-links.ts`;
  - the pages with the most impressions, which are the ones with the most authority to pass on.
- **Guards against:** publishing pages that nothing links to, and treating links as an afterthought.

**3. Howard Moskowitz, psychophysicist** (Prego and Vlasic research; later Mind Genomics)
- **Idea:** there is no single perfect product, only the right product for each group of people. People cannot reliably say what they want, so you find the groups by testing variations and watching behavior.
- **Question he forces:** which group of searchers is this for, what is the evidence they exist, and which new groups is the site not serving yet?
- **Evidence here:**
  - sort GSC and Bing queries into mindsets;
  - `topic-demand.ts`.
- **Guards against:** writing for "everyone", and asking the owner or a model what people want instead of looking.

**4. Clayton Christensen, Harvard Business School** (Jobs to Be Done)
- **Idea:** people "hire" a product to make progress in a specific situation. Understand the job and the circumstances, not the demographic.
- **Question he forces:** what job is the searcher hiring this page for, and which jobs, once one is proven, could the same page shape do again? Examples: finding a portal, looking up a record by address, passing an inspection, getting insured.
- **Evidence here:**
  - query wording such as "how to", "by address", "portal", "will it", "should I";
  - the brief's `who`, `want` and `achieve`.
- **Guards against:** pages about a topic that do not do the job.

**5. Jonah Berger, Wharton** (Contagious; the STEPPS framework)
- **Idea:** content spreads when it offers social currency, gets triggered by everyday cues, carries emotion, is publicly visible, has practical value, and sits inside a story.
- **Question he forces:** why would a real person pass this on or link to it, and to whom? Examples: an agent sending it to a client, a plumber citing it, a journalist using the data.
- **Evidence here:** backlinks, shares and comments on posts, and AI citations.
- **Guards against:** content nobody has a reason to share or link to. On a domain this young, that means it earns no authority.

**6. Byron Sharp, Ehrenberg-Bass Institute** (How Brands Grow)
- **Idea:** brands grow by being easy to think of (mental availability) and easy to find (physical availability), across many light, occasional buyers. Consistent distinctive assets matter more than clever differentiation.
- **Question he forces:** does this put "Before Regret" in front of more of the people who will one day buy a house, consistently?
- **Evidence here:**
  - brand queries in GSC and Bing;
  - AI answers to "what is before regret";
  - consistency of profiles and pages.
- **Guards against:** chasing a few loyal fans instead of reaching many occasional buyers, and a new tagline every week.

**7. Peter Pirolli & Stuart Card, Xerox PARC** (Information Foraging Theory). This seat also carries Herbert Simon's satisficing and Daniel Kahneman's glance test.
- **Idea:** people hunt information by following **scent**, the cues in a title, snippet or link that predict a payoff. They take the first result that looks good enough, and they judge it in one glance.
- **Question they force:**
  - do the title and quick answer promise exactly what the page delivers?
  - would a searcher click it over the results around it, and stop searching once they have?
  - is the one-glance conclusion true?
- **Evidence here:**
  - CTR at a stable position;
  - `fetchQueriesForPage` against the title;
  - the title and quick_answer read in isolation.
- **Guards against:** weak-scent titles, buried answers, and misleading-at-a-glance framing. **Hard limit:** a glance must be made accurate, never exploitable. The owner rejected fear-driven content as "gross".

**8. Ronny Kohavi, experimentation lead at Microsoft (including Bing), Amazon and Airbnb** (co-author of *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments*)
- **Idea:** most ideas do not move the metric they were meant to move, so an organisation learns fastest by running many cheap experiments and trusting the results, not opinions. The same discipline warns against peeking early and calling a winner on noise.
- **Question he forces:** how many bets can we run at once without them contaminating each other, when is each one read, and is the movement bigger than this site's normal week-to-week swing?
- **Evidence here:**
  - weekly series rather than overlapping windows;
  - the 2–3 day GSC lag;
  - Google's ~5-week delay before showing new pages;
  - `data/experiments/` baselines.
- **Guards against:**
  - one slow test at a time when ten could run;
  - declaring a result on 17 days of data, as happened with the county restores on 2026-09-21 (reversed by 2026-10-02).

### The guardrail

**9. Richard Feynman, physicist** ("Cargo Cult Science", 1974)
- **Idea:** ✓ *"The first principle is that you must not fool yourself — and you are the easiest person to fool."* Report the evidence against your idea as carefully as the evidence for it.
- **Question he forces:** what would show this is wrong? Did we check what we are about to claim?
- **Evidence here:**
  - a browser check of every portal, code section and figure;
  - the `assert-*` gates;
  - the page Google actually renders.
- **Guards against:** confident claims from memory, inflated projections, and "verified" that was not. **His role is to keep the growth honest, not to slow it.** He checks the facts inside the plan; he does not veto the plan.

## The procedure

1. **State the growth goal and the metric that decides it.** Use impressions, clicks, ranking positions or index state from GSC or Bing, or citations from the backlink ledger. Never a feeling.

1b. **Size the prize before debating details.** Rank page types and clusters by impressions per page and clicks per page over the last 28 days. Name the engine and how much room it has left: how many more pages of that kind could honestly exist. Project the impact of each candidate move from those per-page numbers, label it as a projection, and state how many weeks it takes before Google shows new pages (~5).

2. **Pull first-party evidence before anyone speaks.** No paid or third-party SEO tools: OpenSEO and DataForSEO are retired. Use what applies:
   - `scripts/pull-gsc-queries.ts`
   - `scripts/pull-bing-queries.ts`
   - `scripts/topic-demand.ts "phrase"`
   - `fetchPagePerformance` / `fetchQueriesForPage` / `fetchUrlInspection`
   - a US results page (`gl=us`) checked by a person, or by the in-app browser with no bot-check bypassing, recorded with `record-observation.ts`

3. **Convene the council.** Each relevant member gives a verdict in two or three lines: their question, what the evidence says, and what they would do. **Skip members with nothing real to add, and say so.** Nine forced opinions is theatre.

4. **Name the disagreements.** Brynjolfsson wants fifty pages while Kohavi wants them read in batches. Berger wants shareability while Feynman objects that a claim is unverified. The disagreement is usually where the insight is.

5. **Decide, as a growth plan:**
   - **the engine move:** scale what already works, with a batch size and a weekly pace;
   - **adjacent bets and long shots**, each its own experiment;
   - for each: the metric, the projected impact, the read date (~5 weeks for anything Google must newly show), the stop condition written before acting, and what you will do if it fails.

   A single tiny test is never the whole plan unless the owner asked for one.

6. **Hand off** to the execution skill. Record the experiment in memory with its read date. For articles, `write-guide` takes the DECISION block as its input and maps each member's verdict into the brief and the draft (its "Coming from the research council" section). Make the verdicts concrete enough to land there: the job, the scent warning, the list of facts to verify, the read date, and which strong pages will link to it.

## Output format

```
## GROWTH GOAL & DECIDING METRIC
## EVIDENCE (first-party, dated)
## THE GROWTH MATH   (engine, per-page numbers, room left, projected impact — labelled projections)
## THE COUNCIL
  Moskowitz — …   (only the members who add something)
## WHERE THEY DISAGREE
## THE PLAN
  Engine move · Adjacent bets · Long shots
  each: Metric · Projected impact · Pace · Read on · Stop condition · If it fails
## HANDOFF → <skill>
```

Keep it short enough to read in two minutes. The council exists to make the site bigger, not to make one page perfect.

## Standing guardrails

**Owner rules** — things the owner actually said; the council cannot override them:

- **Never invent a statistic, keyword or volume,** and never state a monthly search volume that is not in a capture dated before 2026-09-27.
- **No fear-bait, gotchas or engagement tricks.** The owner called a fear-driven post "gross". Prefer curiosity, reassurance and real questions.
- **Contractors are an audience:** they buy ads and they cite the site. Judge pages written for them on citations, not only impressions.
- **Don't disturb current ranking or indexing.** Any change to a live page must not degrade or change its authority, ranking or indexing. In practice this means never changing the slug of a published page.

**Working conventions** — Claude's own, or taken from the content standard. Apply them, but never present them to the owner as owner rules:

- **Pace stays slow** (content standard §1). Publish a few pages a week at most, and never fill one headline formula many times over.
- **Restoring a removed URL vs publishing a new one is a judgement call, not a rule.** Weigh the old URL's history (impressions, links, how long it was live) against a slug that better fits the new page.
- **Never quote the council members in published content.** They shape the thinking; the reader never needs their names.
- **If a member's idea is uncertain, paraphrase it more cautiously** rather than sharpening it into a claim.

Before telling the owner something is "our rule", check which list it is in.
